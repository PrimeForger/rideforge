# RideForge System Architecture

> This document provides a high-level overview of the RideForge backend architecture. It explains the responsibilities of each subsystem, how they interact, and the architectural principles governing the system. This document intentionally avoids implementation details. Those are documented in the respective subsystem documents.

---

# Purpose

RideForge is designed as a modular, event-driven dispatch platform.

The architecture separates responsibilities into independent subsystems that communicate through well-defined boundaries.

This enables:

- Independent evolution of components
- Easier testing
- Better maintainability
- Reduced coupling
- Improved scalability
- Minimal future refactoring

Every subsystem has one clearly defined responsibility.

# Core Architectural Layers

RideForge is organised into several logical layers.

## 1. API Layer

Responsible for:

- HTTP APIs
- WebSocket communication
- Authentication
- Request validation

This layer contains no business logic.

---

## 2. Application Layer

Responsible for:

- Use case orchestration
- Transaction boundaries
- Event publication
- Coordination between subsystems

Examples:

- RideService
- DriverService
- MatchingEngine

---

## 3. Dispatch Layer

Responsible for resolving the applicable dispatch strategy, discovering eligible driver candidates, applying strategy-specific prioritization, ranking candidates where applicable, and selecting a driver for a ride.

The Dispatch Layer is the heart of RideForge.

The dispatch flow is intentionally separated into two concerns:

```text
Dispatch Strategy Resolution

↓

Strategy Execution

↓

Candidate Discovery

↓

Eligibility / Candidate Pipeline

↓

Strategy-Specific Prioritization

↓

Ranking / Selection

↓

Driver Assignment / Offer
```

The selected dispatch strategy does **not automatically define a hard candidate boundary**.

Candidate discovery may expand to nearby drivers or nearby locations when the configured dispatch flow requires it.

---

## 4. Domain Layer

Contains business entities.

Examples:

- Ride
- Driver
- Offer

Domain entities should not know infrastructure details.

---

## 5. Infrastructure Layer

Responsible for external systems.

Examples:

- PostgreSQL
- Redis
- Kafka
- Push Notifications

Infrastructure implements interfaces owned by the application.

# Dispatch Engine

The Dispatch Engine is composed of multiple specialised subsystems.

## Dispatch Strategy Resolution

Before dispatch execution begins, the system resolves the effective dispatch strategy for the ride.

The strategy is determined from the applicable hierarchical configuration.

```text
Most Specific Configuration
        ↓
Explicit Strategy?
   ├── YES → Use strategy
   └── NO
        ↓
Parent Configuration
        ↓
Continue upward
        ↓
System Default
```

The first explicit strategy found takes precedence.

The hierarchy may contain levels such as:

```text
State
 ↓
District
 ↓
City / Town
 ↓
Rural Area
 ↓
Auto Stand
 ↓
Ride-level / Other configured level
```

The hierarchy must remain extensible and must not be implemented as a hard-coded list of geographic levels.

The architectural rule is:

> Specific configuration overrides inherited configuration.

Strategy resolution determines **which dispatch strategy applies**. It does not determine the complete candidate pool.

## Primary Dispatch Strategies

RideForge supports two primary dispatch strategies.

### Smart Stand Dispatch

Smart Stand Dispatch is **stand-preferred, not stand-exclusive**.

When the pickup location is within the configured radius of an auto stand, the relevant stand's eligible drivers receive the initial preference according to stand queue/order rules.

If suitable stand drivers are unavailable or unsuitable, the dispatch flow may expand to other eligible drivers, including:

- Drivers from nearby stands
- Drivers outside any stand
- Drivers associated with nearby locations

When the pickup location is outside all configured stand radii, the dispatch flow considers eligible nearby drivers without restricting candidates to auto-stand drivers.

### Smart Dispatch

Smart Dispatch is stand-agnostic.

It considers eligible nearby drivers without using auto-stand membership as a dispatch preference.

Drivers may be considered regardless of whether they are:

- At an auto stand
- Outside an auto stand
- Associated with another nearby location

The candidate selection is based on the established eligibility, distance, ETA, availability, ranking, and other configured dispatch factors.

## Cross-Location Dispatch

Location boundaries do not automatically form hard dispatch boundaries.

If the originating location cannot provide a suitable eligible driver, discovery may expand to nearby locations.

When a candidate belongs to another configured location, that candidate's location-level dispatch strategy can provide the strategy context for how that candidate is prioritized.

For example:

```text
Location A
Smart Dispatch
    ↓
No suitable local driver
    ↓
Expand discovery
    ↓
Location B
Smart Stand Dispatch
    ↓
Evaluate eligible drivers from B
    ↓
Compare eligible candidates
```

Cross-location consideration remains subject to:

- Driver eligibility
- Availability
- Distance / ETA
- Regional and legal constraints
- Service constraints
- Other dispatch constraints

Therefore:

```text
Dispatch Strategy
        ≠
Candidate Discovery Boundary
```

and:

```text
Location Boundary
        ≠
Automatic Driver Ineligibility
```

## Strategy Execution vs Candidate Discovery

These responsibilities must remain separate.

```text
Configuration Hierarchy
        ↓
Resolve Effective Strategy
        ↓
Candidate Discovery Scope
        ↓
Eligibility Filtering
        ↓
Strategy-Specific Prioritization
        ↓
Ranking / Comparison
        ↓
Assignment
```

The configuration hierarchy determines **which strategy applies**.

Candidate discovery determines **which drivers enter the candidate flow**.

The resolved strategy determines **how those candidates are prioritized and dispatched**.

The ranking engine determines comparable candidate scores where ranking is applicable.


## Discovery Engine

Purpose

Find eligible driver candidates within the applicable discovery scope.

Responsibilities

- H3 lookup
- Density-aware expansion
- Candidate discovery
- Geo refinement
- Stand-aware initial discovery where required
- Broader candidate expansion when the preferred stand cannot provide a suitable driver
- Nearby-location expansion for cross-location dispatch

Outputs

Candidate IDs with sufficient location/context information for downstream processing.

The Discovery Engine must not interpret Smart Stand Dispatch as a stand-only candidate boundary.

---

## Candidate Pipeline

Purpose

Transform discovered candidates into eligible and dispatchable candidates.

Responsibilities

- Batch loading
- Validation
- Filtering
- Eligibility checks
- Enrichment
- Strategy-specific candidate handling
- Ranking preparation
- Heap construction where applicable

Outputs

Candidates prepared for prioritization, ranking, and assignment.

The Candidate Pipeline must preserve enough context to distinguish:

- Candidate eligibility
- Stand membership / stand preference
- Candidate location
- Dispatch strategy context
- Distance / ETA and other ranking factors

---

## Ranking Engine

Purpose

Calculate a comparable score for every candidate.

Responsibilities

- Feature extraction
- Travel calculation
- Quality calculation
- Fairness calculation
- Score normalization

Outputs

Candidate score.

---

## Retry Policy

Purpose

Determine dispatch behaviour.

Responsibilities

- Search radius
- Candidate limit
- Offer batch size
- Offer timeout

Retry Policy owns dispatch decisions.

---

## Matching Engine

Purpose

Coordinate the entire dispatch workflow.

Responsibilities

- Start dispatch
- Resolve the effective dispatch strategy
- Execute candidate discovery
- Execute the candidate pipeline
- Apply strategy-specific dispatch flow
- Reserve drivers
- Publish driver offers

The Matching Engine does **not** contain ranking logic.

The Matching Engine does **not** contain discovery implementation logic.

The Matching Engine orchestrates the dispatch workflow.

# Architectural Dependency Direction

Dependencies always flow inward.

```text
API

↓

Application

↓

Dispatch

↓

Domain

↓

Infrastructure
```

Never the opposite.

Infrastructure implements interfaces.

Application owns interfaces.

# Package Ownership

Every package owns exactly one responsibility.

| Package   | Responsibility         |
| --------- | ---------------------- |
| discovery | Candidate discovery    |
| candidate | Candidate lifecycle    |
| ranking   | Candidate scoring      |
| matching  | Dispatch orchestration |
| retry     | Dispatch policy        |
| geo       | Geographic utilities   |
| realtime  | Driver communication   |
| outbox    | Event publication      |

No responsibility should be duplicated.

# Event-Driven Communication

RideForge uses asynchronous events for cross-component communication.

Examples:

```
RideCreated

↓

MatchingStarted

↓

DriverOffered

↓

DriverAccepted

↓

RideAssigned
```

Events are persisted before publication using the Outbox Pattern.

# Design Principles

RideForge follows several architectural principles.

## Single Responsibility

Each package owns one concern.

---

## Explicit Boundaries

Packages communicate only through well-defined interfaces.

---

## Low Coupling

Subsystems should not know implementation details of other subsystems.

---

## High Cohesion

Related logic stays together.

---

## Production First

Every implementation should be deployable.

Temporary code should not exist.

---

## Incremental Evolution

Architecture grows in stages.

No subsystem should require large rewrites to support future capabilities.

# Dispatch Architecture Invariants

The following rules are architectural invariants for the dispatch system:

1. **The most specific explicit dispatch configuration wins.**
2. **Higher-level dispatch configuration is inherited only when no more specific explicit configuration exists.**
3. **The configuration hierarchy must support extensible/intermediate levels and must not be hard-coded to a fixed geographic hierarchy.**
4. **Smart Stand Dispatch is stand-preferred, not stand-exclusive.**
5. **Smart Dispatch is stand-agnostic.**
6. **A stand radius is not the same thing as the general driver discovery radius or cross-location expansion radius.**
7. **Dispatch strategy resolution and candidate discovery are separate responsibilities.**
8. **A dispatch strategy must not automatically create a hard candidate boundary.**
9. **Cross-location candidate discovery is allowed when the configured dispatch flow requires broader supply.**
10. **Cross-location candidates remain subject to eligibility, availability, legal, regional, service, and other hard constraints.**
11. **AI-assisted dispatch is an optimization capability, not a third primary dispatch strategy.**
12. **AI cannot override hard eligibility, legal, safety, or authoritative domain constraints.**
13. **Retry/fallback behaviour must not silently replace the resolved strategy unless explicitly configured.**

These invariants should remain consistent across business rules, component architecture, ADRs, diagrams, and implementation.

# Summary

RideForge is organised as a collection of independent, production-grade subsystems.

Each subsystem owns one responsibility.

Each subsystem evolves independently.

The architecture is intentionally designed to minimise future refactoring while supporting long-term scalability and maintainability.
