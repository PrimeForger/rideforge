# RideForge Geo Engine

> **Document:** `docs/02-architecture/GEO_ENGINE.md`  
> **Status:** Active architecture documentation  
> **Scope:** Geographic indexing, H3, Redis GEO refinement, coordinate handling, distance refinement, travel/ETA boundaries, and future routing integration  
> **Audience:** Backend developers, architects, reviewers, and AI coding agents

---

# 1. Executive Summary

The RideForge Geo Engine provides the geographic capabilities required by candidate discovery and dispatch.

Its responsibility is to answer geographic questions efficiently:

- Which spatial cells are relevant to a pickup?
- Which drivers are geographically relevant to the pickup?
- Which nearby locations/cells should be considered when discovery expands?
- Which candidates are geographically close enough to continue?
- What geographic/travel information can ranking consume?

The Geo Engine provides **candidate discovery primitives and geographic refinement**. It does not decide which dispatch strategy applies to a ride and does not decide the final driver.

The core architecture is:

```text
Ride Pickup
    |
    v
Resolved Dispatch Context
    |
    v
Discovery Policy
    |
    v
H3 Spatial Index
    |
    v
Candidate Discovery
    |
    v
Optional Geo Refinement
    |
    v
Candidate Set + Geographic Context
    |
    v
Travel / ETA Features
    |
    v
Strategy-specific Prioritization / Ranking
```

The key principles are:

> **Use inexpensive spatial indexing for coarse discovery, then use more precise geographic or routing calculations only where they provide meaningful value.**

> **Dispatch strategy influences candidate prioritization and preferred discovery sources, but it does not automatically define a hard geographic candidate boundary.**

# 2. Purpose

The Geo Engine provides geographic primitives and infrastructure used by:

- Candidate Discovery
- Candidate Refinement
- Ranking
- Driver Location Management
- Future ETA calculation
- Cross-location geographic expansion

It does not own:

- Dispatch strategy resolution
- Hierarchical configuration inheritance
- Driver eligibility
- Final driver selection
- Stand queue policy
- Dispatch retry policy

The Geo Engine may receive a resolved dispatch context so discovery can apply the appropriate geographic search policy, but it must not resolve the configuration hierarchy itself.

# 3. Goals

## 3.1 Fast spatial lookup

Geographic candidate discovery is on the dispatch critical path. The system must find nearby drivers without scanning the entire driver population.

## 3.2 Hierarchical spatial partitioning

H3 provides a stable spatial representation for drivers and ride locations.

```text
Latitude + Longitude
        |
        v
       H3
        |
        v
    Cell Index
```

## 3.3 Bounded geographic work

Geo operations must remain bounded by:

- search radius,
- candidate limit,
- ring budget,
- cell budget,
- refinement requirements.

## 3.4 Separation of coarse and precise operations

The architecture distinguishes:

```text
Coarse spatial indexing
```

from:

```text
Precise geographic/travel calculation
```

---

# 4. Non-Goals

The Geo Engine does not own:

- candidate ranking,
- driver quality scoring,
- driver reservation,
- offer publication,
- retry policy,
- ride lifecycle management.

# 7. H3 as the Primary Spatial Index

H3 is the primary spatial indexing mechanism for dispatch discovery.

It converts latitude/longitude into a spatial cell and provides neighborhood relationships suitable for ring-based search.

```text
Earth
  |
  v
H3 Grid
  |
  v
Cell
  |
  v
Driver Membership
```

H3 is an index, not a final distance or ETA calculation.

---

# 8. H3 Resolution

The application configures H3 resolution. Discovery components should consume the configured service rather than embedding a resolution constant.

```text
H3 Configuration
    |
    v
Resolution
    |
    v
H3 Service
```

---

# 9. H3 Service

The H3 service owns H3-specific geographic operations, including:

- coordinate-to-cell conversion,
- neighboring-cell calculation,
- ring generation,
- H3-related geographic primitives.

Discovery policy must not be embedded into these low-level operations.

---

# 10. H3 Driver Index

The H3 Driver Index associates active drivers with their current H3 cell.

```text
Driver
  |
  v
Current Coordinates
  |
  v
H3 Cell
  |
  v
Redis Cell Membership
```

Discovery performs the reverse lookup:

```text
Pickup Coordinates
      |
      v
Pickup H3 Cell
      |
      v
Neighboring Cells
      |
      v
Driver IDs
```

---

# 11. H3 Index Is Not the Source of Truth

H3 membership does not prove driver eligibility.

A driver can move, disconnect, become busy, or be reserved after its spatial membership is written.

Therefore:

```text
H3 membership != Driver eligibility
```

Later filtering and reservation remain authoritative.

---

# 12. Driver Location Flow

```text
Driver GPS Update
      |
      v
Driver Location Service
      |
      v
Geo Service
      |
      +-- coordinate validation
      +-- H3 cell calculation
      |
      v
Driver Cache
      |
      v
H3 Driver Index
```

The location service coordinates updates, the H3 service performs geographic calculations, and the index maintains spatial membership.

---

# 13. Cell Movement

A driver moving between cells requires consistent membership handling:

```text
Old Cell
   |
   v
Driver Movement
   |
   v
New Cell
```

The desired state transition is:

```text
Remove old membership
        +
Add new membership
        +
Update current-cell mapping
        +
Refresh required TTL/state
```

---

# 14. Same-Cell Updates

GPS updates frequently occur while a driver remains in the same H3 cell.

```text
Old Cell == New Cell
        |
        v
Refresh required state
        |
        v
Avoid full cell movement
```

This reduces Redis writes and unnecessary index work.

---

# 15. Atomic Cell Movement

Cell movement is concurrency-sensitive. Related Redis operations should be atomic where practical.

A Lua script is an appropriate implementation when multiple Redis commands must form one state transition:

```text
Driver Movement
      |
      v
Atomic Redis Operation
      |
      +-- Remove old cell membership
      +-- Add new cell membership
      +-- Update current-cell mapping
      +-- Refresh required state
```

---

# 16. Empty Cell Cleanup

When a driver leaves a cell, the index should avoid retaining orphaned empty sets.

```text
Remove driver
     |
     v
Cell empty?
     |
     +-- No  -> keep cell
     |
     +-- Yes -> delete cell key
```

---

# 17. Cell Lookup

Discovery may need multiple cells. Redis operations should be batched/pipelined where appropriate.

Prefer:

```text
Cell 1 --+
Cell 2 --+--> Redis Pipeline
Cell 3 --+
```

over repeated sequential network round trips.

---

# 18. Progressive H3 Search

Discovery uses progressive ring expansion:

```text
Initial Search Scope
        |
        v
Enough suitable candidates?
      /   \
    YES    NO
     |      |
    Stop    v
         Expand spatial scope
              |
              v
          Next H3 ring / area
              |
              v
         Enough candidates?
              |
             ...
```

The Geo Engine supplies spatial primitives. The Discovery Engine owns the expansion policy.

Expansion may be required because:

```text
Local supply is unavailable
Preferred stand has no suitable driver
Candidate set is below the required threshold
Cross-location search is allowed
```

The Geo Engine must not infer these business reasons by itself.

# 19. Important Boundary

The Geo Engine should not decide how many rings a ride should search.

That is dispatch/discovery policy.

The Geo Engine answers questions such as:

```text
Which cells belong to this ring?
```

---

# 20. Redis GEO

Redis GEO can act as a secondary geographic refinement mechanism.

```text
H3 / Spatial Discovery
 |
 v
Coarse Candidate IDs
 |
 v
Redis GEO
 |
 v
Refined Candidates
```

It is not the primary spatial index in the current architecture.

For Smart Stand Dispatch, Redis GEO may help refine candidates discovered around:

```text
Preferred stand
Nearby stands
Broader nearby-driver search
Nearby locations
```

The refinement mechanism must not convert the stand preference into a stand-only candidate boundary.

# 21. Why Redis GEO Is Secondary

H3 is used for:

- spatial partitioning,
- ring expansion,
- cell-based discovery,
- future regional ownership.

Redis GEO is useful for:

- radius refinement,
- geographic distance filtering,
- secondary spatial lookup.

The two mechanisms are complementary.

# 22. Geo Refinement Boundary

Refinement is isolated behind an abstraction.

```text
Candidate Search Result
        |
        v
      Refiner
        |
        +-- No-op Refiner
        |
        +-- Redis GEO Refiner
```

Redis-specific implementation must remain behind this boundary.

# 23. No-op Refinement

A no-op implementation is useful when refinement is disabled or unnecessary.

```text
Candidates
   |
   v
Noop Refiner
   |
   v
Same Candidates
```

It avoids spreading optional-refinement conditionals through discovery code.

---

# 24. Redis GEO Refinement

Redis GEO should refine only the candidate set already produced by coarse discovery.

```text
H3 Candidate Set
      |
      v
Redis GEO Lookup
      |
      v
Radius Refinement
      |
      v
Refined Candidate Set
```

This avoids querying the entire driver population.

---

# 25. Haversine Distance

Haversine calculates straight-line geographic distance between two coordinates.

```text
Coordinates A + Coordinates B
             |
             v
          Haversine
             |
             v
       Distance in km
```

It is useful for precise geographic radius checks but is not road travel time.

---

# 26. Haversine Refinement Position

The intended sequence is:

```text
H3
 |
 v
Candidate Set
 |
 v
Haversine
 |
 v
Precise Geographic Radius
```

Do not calculate Haversine against every driver in the system.

---

# 27. Haversine vs Routing ETA

These represent different measurements.

| Measurement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| H3 | Spatial index/cell relationship |
| Haversine | Straight-line geographic distance |
| Road distance | Distance along a road network |
| ETA | Expected travel time |

A driver can have a short Haversine distance and a long road ETA because of roads, traffic, barriers, or route geometry.

---

# 28. Geo Refinement vs Ranking

Geo refinement answers:

> Is this candidate geographically relevant?

Ranking answers:

> Which geographically relevant candidate is preferable?

Therefore:

```text
H3
 |
 v
Geo Refinement
 |
 v
Ranking
```

Ranking formulas must not be embedded in refinement.

---

# 29. Travel Calculation Boundary

Travel information belongs behind a travel calculation boundary.

```text
Candidate
   |
   v
Travel Calculator
   |
   +-- Distance
   +-- ETA
   |
   v
Travel Features
```

This allows Ranking to consume travel information without depending directly on a routing vendor.

# 32. Routing Performance

Routing is significantly more expensive than H3 lookup.

Therefore:

```text
H3
 |
 v
Small candidate set
 |
 v
Geo refinement
 |
 v
Routing / ETA
```

The system must not route every driver globally.

---

# 33. Bounded Routing

If routing is introduced, routing work must be bounded by mechanisms such as:

- candidate limit,
- top-K selection,
- provider batch limits,
- controlled concurrency,
- provider timeout,
- provider quota,
- explicit fallback behaviour.

These controls belong to travel/routing and dispatch configuration, not H3 primitives.

---

# 34. Coordinate Validation

Geographic components must validate coordinates before geographic calculations.

Latitude:

```text
-90 <= latitude <= 90
```

Longitude:

```text
-180 <= longitude <= 180
```

Invalid coordinates should be rejected rather than silently transformed.

---

# 35. Coordinate Precision

Coordinates should retain sufficient precision for the operation being performed.

Do not unnecessarily round latitude/longitude before:

- H3 conversion,
- distance calculation,
- routing.

If normalization is required for cache keys or storage, it must be explicit.

---

# 36. Geographic Units

Units must be explicit and consistent.

```text
Latitude       = degrees
Longitude      = degrees
Radius         = kilometers
Distance       = kilometers
ETA            = seconds
```

Do not mix meters and kilometers without explicit conversion.

---

# 37. Radius Handling

Radius values are discovery inputs supplied by dispatch/discovery policy.

```text
Dispatch / Discovery Policy
        |
        +-- Stand Radius
        +-- Driver Search Radius
        +-- Location Expansion Radius
        |
        v
Geo Engine
        |
        v
Geographic Search
```

The Geo Engine consumes these values and performs the requested geographic operations. It does not independently change dispatch radius policy.

These radii must not be assumed to be identical.

For example:

```text
Stand Radius
    = determines whether pickup is inside a configured stand area

Driver Search Radius
    = determines nearby driver discovery

Location Expansion Radius
    = determines how far cross-location discovery may expand
```

The exact policy belongs to the Dispatch / Discovery layers.

# 38. Search Radius vs Refinement Radius

The architecture should distinguish:

```text
Stand Radius
Driver Search Radius
Location Expansion Radius
```

from:

```text
Precise Geo Refinement Radius
```

They serve different purposes and must not be silently changed or mixed across units.

In particular:

```text
rider inside stand radius
```

is a **strategy-context condition** for Smart Stand Dispatch.

It is not equivalent to:

```text
only search drivers inside the stand
```

Likewise, a cross-location expansion radius is a candidate discovery boundary, not a dispatch-strategy override.

# 39. Geo Strategy

The discovery architecture supports geographic search strategies alongside H3.

```text
CandidateSearcher
      |
      +-- H3-based Strategy
      |
      +-- Geographic Refinement Strategy
```

These are **geographic discovery mechanisms**, not business dispatch strategies.

Do not confuse:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
Smart Dispatch
```

with:

```text
H3 Search
Redis GEO
Other Geo Search Mechanisms
```

Dispatch strategy is resolved from the configuration hierarchy. Geo strategy determines how the geographic candidate search is technically executed.

# 40. Strategy Selection

Bootstrap/configuration chooses the active geographic search implementation.

```text
Infrastructure / Geo Configuration
        |
        v
Candidate Searcher
        |
        +-- H3-based discovery
        |
        +-- Geographic refinement
```

This technical selection is separate from ride-level dispatch strategy resolution.

The ride-level strategy is resolved through the hierarchical dispatch configuration:

```text
Most Specific Explicit Configuration
        |
        v
Effective Dispatch Strategy
        |
        v
Discovery Context
        |
        v
Candidate Searcher / Geo Engine
```

Application services should not hard-code the configuration hierarchy or embed geographic implementation selection throughout business logic.

# 42. Real-Time Nature of Geo State

Driver geographic state is operational and can temporarily differ between GPS state, cache state, and H3 index state.

Therefore discovery must tolerate stale candidates.

Later filtering and reservation provide the safety boundary.

---

# 43. Reservation Is Not a Geo Operation

The Geo Engine must never become responsible for driver reservation.

```text
Geo
 |
 v
Spatial relevance
```

versus:

```text
Matching
 |
 v
Reservation
```

# 43.1 Dispatch-Strategy Geographic Semantics

The Geo Engine must support the geographic semantics of both dispatch strategies without owning the strategy decision.

## Smart Stand Dispatch

When the pickup is inside a configured stand radius:

```text
Pickup
  |
  v
Inside Stand Radius
  |
  v
Preferred Stand Context
  |
  v
Discover / refine preferred stand candidates first
```

If the preferred stand cannot provide a suitable candidate, discovery may broaden:

```text
Preferred Stand
      |
      v
Nearby Stand / Non-Stand Drivers
      |
      v
Nearby Locations
      |
      v
Broader Eligible Candidate Set
```

The Geo Engine must not assume that:

```text
inside stand radius
=
search only inside stand
```

## Smart Dispatch

For Smart Dispatch:

```text
Pickup
  |
  v
Nearby Geographic Search
  |
  v
Eligible Candidates
```

Stand membership is not a geographic preference supplied by Smart Dispatch.

## Cross-Location Dispatch

When local supply is insufficient, the Discovery Engine may expand into nearby locations.

The Geo Engine provides the geographic operations needed for that expansion:

```text
Origin Location
      |
      v
Local Search
      |
      v
No suitable supply
      |
      v
Nearby Location Search
      |
      v
Candidates from nearby locations
```

The candidate's source location and stand context should remain available to downstream components so the applicable strategy-specific prioritization can be performed correctly.

## Hard Boundaries

The following remain independent concerns:

```text
Legal / Regional Eligibility
Dispatch Strategy Resolution
Driver Eligibility
Geographic Search Scope
Strategy-specific Prioritization
Final Ranking
```

Geographic proximity alone must never make an otherwise ineligible driver assignable.

---

# 44. Geo Failure Handling

## H3 failure

Return an error. Do not fabricate a cell.

## Redis index failure

Return/propagate the infrastructure error according to discovery failure policy and expose it through observability.

## Refinement failure

If refinement is required, surface the failure. If optional, fallback must be explicitly configured.

## Routing failure

Routing failure must not corrupt geographic index state. Any fallback must be explicitly defined by travel/ranking policy.

# 47. Performance Model

The desired model is:

```text
O(spatial cells)
        +
O(candidate refinement)
        +
O(small candidate routing)
```

rather than:

```text
O(all drivers)
```

The system should progressively reduce the candidate set before expensive operations.

---

# 48. Progressive Cost Reduction

```text
All Drivers
    |
    | H3
    v
Nearby Drivers
    |
    | GEO / Haversine
    v
Precise Candidates
    |
    | ETA
    v
Rankable Candidates
```

Each step should operate on a smaller set than the previous one whenever practical.

---

# 49. Avoiding N+1 Geographic Calls

Avoid unbounded per-candidate external calls.

If routing is unavoidable per candidate, use explicit concurrency and provider limits. Prefer batch APIs where supported.

# 51. Geographic Invariants

Important invariants:

### Coordinate validity

Invalid coordinates must not enter geographic calculations.

### Index consistency

A successful movement should not intentionally leave the driver in its previous cell.

### Bounded search

Discovery must respect configured limits.

### Unit consistency

Distance and radius units must be explicit.

### Separation

Geo code must not make dispatch decisions.

# 52. Common Implementation Mistakes

## Mistake 1 — Treating H3 distance as ETA

H3 is a spatial index, not a routing engine.

## Mistake 2 — Scanning all drivers

Never replace spatial discovery with a global driver scan.

## Mistake 3 — Making Redis GEO the only index

Current architecture is:

```text
H3 = primary discovery
Redis GEO = secondary refinement
```

## Mistake 4 — Routing before spatial reduction

Prefer:

```text
H3 -> refinement -> routing
```

not:

```text
all drivers -> routing
```

## Mistake 5 — Putting retry policy inside Geo

Radius and expansion policy belong to dispatch/discovery policy.

## Mistake 6 — Putting ranking inside Geo

Geo determines geographic relevance; Ranking determines candidate preference.

## Mistake 7 — Hard-coded geographic configuration

Do not hard-code H3 resolution, search radius, thresholds, or routing timeouts inside business components.

## Mistake 8 — Treating Smart Stand Dispatch as stand-only

Do not filter the entire candidate set to stand drivers merely because the pickup is inside a stand radius.

Correct:

```text
Inside stand radius
    |
    v
Prefer stand candidates
    |
    v
No suitable stand candidate
    |
    v
Broaden candidate discovery
```

## Mistake 9 — Treating dispatch strategy as a geographic boundary

A location configured for Smart Stand Dispatch does not automatically prevent its eligible drivers from serving a ride originating in another nearby location.

## Mistake 10 — Resolving hierarchical configuration inside Geo

The Geo Engine must not implement:

```text
State -> District -> Town -> Rural Area -> Stand -> Ride
```

configuration inheritance.

The effective dispatch strategy must already be resolved before geographic discovery uses the resulting context.

## Mistake 11 — Confusing stand radius with driver search radius

A stand radius determines whether a pickup is inside a stand's preferred service area. It does not necessarily define the maximum geographic area from which drivers can be considered.

## Mistake 12 — Discarding candidate location context

Cross-location candidates may need their source location, stand membership, and strategy context preserved for downstream prioritization.

## Rule 1 — H3 Is an Index

Do not treat H3 as a routing engine.

## Rule 2 — Preserve Discovery Boundaries

Do not move ring-expansion or cross-location expansion policy into low-level geographic primitives.

## Rule 3 — Preserve Refinement Boundary

Do not embed Redis GEO directly into unrelated H3 logic.

## Rule 4 — No Global Scans

Never introduce a nearby-driver implementation that scans every driver.

## Rule 5 — Keep Units Explicit

Always make distance/time units obvious.

## Rule 6 — Configuration Over Constants

Do not hard-code operational geographic parameters.

## Rule 7 — Dispatch Strategy Is Resolved Upstream

Do not implement hierarchical dispatch configuration inheritance inside the Geo Engine.

## Rule 8 — Stand Preference Is Not Stand Exclusivity

Do not turn Smart Stand Dispatch into a stand-only geographic search.

## Rule 9 — Preserve Cross-Location Candidates

Do not reject candidates merely because their source location or stand differs from the ride's origin location. Apply the eligibility and dispatch rules supplied by the upstream layers.

## Rule 10 — Preserve Candidate Context

When discovery crosses stand or location boundaries, retain relevant context such as:

```text
source location
stand membership
preferred stand
queue position
discovery source
distance
ETA inputs
```

where the downstream pipeline requires it.

## Rule 11 — Avoid Premature Routing

Do not introduce a routing provider without a concrete requirement, contract, and performance plan.

## Rule 12 — Avoid Premature Geo Caching

Do not create elaborate route caches before routing exists and cache behaviour is justified.

## Rule 13 — Keep Geo Side Effects Clear

Geographic calculations should not mutate driver operational state except where the location/index component explicitly owns that state.

## Rule 14 — Preserve Strategy Separation

Do not conflate:

```text
Dispatch Strategy
Candidate Discovery
Geo Search Mechanism
Candidate Ranking
```

They are separate architectural responsibilities.

# 59. Architectural Decisions

The following decisions should be represented in ADR documentation.

## ADR — H3 as Primary Spatial Index

H3 provides the primary spatial partitioning and discovery mechanism.

## ADR — Redis GEO as Secondary Refinement

Redis GEO refines the candidate set after coarse H3 discovery.

## ADR — Geographic Refinement Boundary

Refinement is isolated behind an abstraction so the underlying mechanism can evolve.

## ADR — Progressive Spatial Search

Search expands progressively rather than unnecessarily scanning a fixed maximum area.

## ADR — Separate Geographic Relevance From Ranking

Geo determines spatial relevance; Ranking determines candidate preference.

## ADR — Routing Behind a Provider Boundary

Future routing providers must not leak provider-specific details into Ranking.

# 65. Final Summary

The RideForge Geo Engine is the geographic foundation underneath candidate discovery and ranking.

Its current architecture is:

```text
Coordinates
    |
    v
H3
    |
    v
Spatial Candidate Discovery
    |
    v
Redis GEO Refinement
    |
    v
Candidate Set
    |
    v
Travel / ETA
    |
    v
Ranking
```

The most important rules are:

1. **H3 is the primary spatial index.**
2. **Redis GEO is a refinement mechanism, not the primary index.**
3. **H3 discovery must remain bounded and progressive.**
4. **Smart Stand Dispatch is stand-preferred, not stand-exclusive.**
5. **Smart Dispatch is stand-agnostic.**
6. **A stand radius determines stand-preference context; it does not automatically define a hard driver search boundary.**
7. **Cross-location discovery is allowed when dispatch/discovery policy requires expansion.**
8. **Candidate source location and stand context must remain available for downstream prioritization.**
9. **Geographic relevance and candidate ranking are separate concerns.**
10. **Dispatch strategy resolution and configuration inheritance do not belong inside the Geo Engine.**
11. **Routing must sit behind a provider boundary.**
12. **Expensive routing should happen only after coarse candidate reduction.**
13. **Coordinates and units must be explicit and validated.**
14. **Geo infrastructure must not own dispatch policy.**
15. **Operational geo state must not be confused with driver eligibility.**
16. **Existing capabilities must be reused instead of duplicated.**

The objective is a geographic subsystem that is fast enough for real-time dispatch today while providing a clean path toward precise distance, traffic-aware ETA, routing, regional H3 ownership, hot-region analysis, and future ML-assisted travel prediction.
