# 1. Purpose

This document defines the RideForge Smart Dispatch AI system.

Smart Dispatch is the intelligence-assisted dispatch strategy used to identify and rank the best eligible driver for a ride.

The system is designed around:

```text
Candidate Discovery
        ↓
Deterministic Eligibility
        ↓
Feature / Signal Resolution
        ↓
AI / Algorithmic Scoring
        ↓
Candidate Ranking
        ↓
Dispatch Recommendation
        ↓
Deterministic Reservation
        ↓
Ride Assignment
```

The AI layer improves the quality of the recommendation.

It does not become the authoritative owner of reservation or ride state.

---

# 2. Smart Dispatch Objective

The objective of Smart Dispatch is:

> **Select the best eligible driver for a ride according to the active dispatch policy and available operational intelligence.**

The objective is not simply:

```text
Nearest Driver
```

It is:

```text
Best Eligible Driver
```

where "best" may consider multiple operational signals.

---

# 3. Hybrid Dispatch Strategy

RideForge supports two primary dispatch strategies:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
+
Smart Dispatch
```

The active strategy is resolved from the applicable hierarchical dispatch configuration before strategy execution.

Conceptually:

```text
Ride Request
      ↓
Hierarchical Dispatch Configuration
      ↓
Effective Dispatch Strategy
      ├── Smart Stand Dispatch
      └── Smart Dispatch
```

The platform is not permanently dependent on Smart Dispatch.

The resolved strategy is a business/operational decision. AI-assisted ranking is an optimization capability that may operate within the resolved strategy; it is not a third primary dispatch strategy.

---

# 4. Smart Stand Dispatch

Smart Stand Dispatch remains useful where queue-based allocation provides:

```text
Predictability
Operational Simplicity
Fair Queue Ordering
Low Computational Cost
Easy Human Understanding
```

Smart Stand Dispatch is **stand-preferred, not stand-exclusive**.

When the rider is inside a configured auto-stand radius, eligible drivers at that stand are considered first and the stand queue/order is applied according to the dispatch policy.

If the preferred stand cannot provide a suitable/available driver, the candidate search may expand to:

```text
Drivers outside the preferred stand
Drivers at nearby stands
Drivers from nearby locations
```

If the rider is outside the radius of all configured auto stands, Smart Stand Dispatch does not create a stand-only candidate pool; eligible nearby drivers may be considered normally.

AI should not silently replace or change the resolved Smart Stand Dispatch strategy.

# 5. Smart Dispatch

Smart Dispatch evaluates eligible candidates using a richer set of signals.

Potential signals include:

```text
Driver Location
Pickup ETA
Distance
Driver Availability
Ride Characteristics
Demand Conditions
Supply Conditions
Historical Operational Signals
Dispatch Policy
```

The exact signals depend on the implemented model and available data.

---

# 6. Core Smart Dispatch Principle

The most important architectural rule is:

```text
AI Recommendation
        ≠
Reservation
```

A model can recommend:

```text
Driver A
```

but the core platform must still determine whether Driver A can actually be reserved.

---

# 7. Dispatch Flow

The complete conceptual flow is:

```text
Ride Request
     ↓
Resolve Hierarchical Dispatch Configuration
     ↓
Effective Dispatch Strategy
     ↓
Strategy-specific Candidate Discovery
     ↓
Eligibility Filtering
     ↓
Feature Resolution
     ↓
AI / Algorithmic Ranking where applicable
     ↓
Candidate Ordering / Strategy-specific Prioritization
     ↓
Reservation Attempt
     ↓
Assignment
```

For Smart Stand Dispatch, the flow first gives preference to the relevant stand when the pickup is inside its configured stand radius. If suitable stand supply is unavailable, candidate discovery may expand without changing the resolved strategy.

For Smart Dispatch, candidate discovery is stand-agnostic.

If AI ranking fails:

```text
AI Failure
    ↓
Deterministic Ranking / Configured Fallback
```

AI failure must not automatically change the effective dispatch strategy.

---

# 8. Candidate Discovery

Candidate Discovery identifies drivers that are potentially relevant to the ride.

Potential discovery signals include:

```text
Geographic Proximity
Availability
Operational Area
Vehicle Type
Driver State
```

Candidate discovery should be efficient enough for real-time dispatch.

---

# 9. Candidate Discovery Is Not Eligibility

Candidate discovery produces:

```text
Potential Candidates
```

It does not guarantee:

```text
Eligible Candidates
```

The system must perform the required deterministic validation before ranking.

---

# 10. Deterministic Eligibility

Eligibility must be evaluated using authoritative system rules.

Examples include:

```text
Driver Online
Driver Available
Vehicle Eligible
Correct Operational Region
Ride Constraints Satisfied
Driver Not Already Reserved
Required Operational Conditions
```

The exact eligibility rules belong to the core dispatch/domain logic.

---

# 11. Regulatory and Regional Rules

AI must not override regulatory or regional restrictions.

The correct flow is:

```text
Candidate
   ↓
Eligibility
   ↓
Regional / Regulatory Validation
   ↓
Eligible?
   ├── No → Remove
   └── Yes → Continue
```

A high AI score never makes an ineligible driver eligible.

---

# 12. Candidate Filtering

The preferred sequence is:

```text
Candidate Discovery
        ↓
Cheap Deterministic Filters
        ↓
Required Eligibility
        ↓
AI / Ranking Features
```

This prevents unnecessary AI computation for candidates that cannot receive the ride.

---

# 13. Candidate Set Size

The ranking layer should receive a manageable candidate set.

The candidate count should be determined by:

```text
Dispatch Latency
Geographic Density
Expected Availability
Ranking Cost
```

Do not assume that evaluating every available driver is always optimal.

---

# 14. Feature Resolution

After eligibility filtering, the system resolves the signals needed for ranking.

Potential feature groups:

```text
Driver Features
Ride Features
Spatial Features
Temporal Features
ETA Features
Demand Features
Supply Features
Historical Features
```

---

# 15. Feature Freshness

Real-time dispatch features must respect their freshness requirements.

Examples:

```text
Driver Location
→ Very Fresh

Availability
→ Very Fresh

ETA
→ Current / Recent

Historical Performance
→ Rolling / Periodic

Demand Forecast
→ Forecast Window Specific
```

Stale information can produce incorrect rankings.

---

# 16. Ranking Objective

The ranking system should produce an ordered list:

```text
Candidate 1
Candidate 2
Candidate 3
...
```

The first candidate is preferred according to the active dispatch policy.

The ranking output is a recommendation, not a transaction.

---

Before introducing ML ranking, RideForge should maintain a deterministic baseline.

A simple baseline can consider:

```text
Pickup ETA
Distance
Availability
Operational Constraints
```

This baseline is important because it provides:

```text
Fallback
Benchmark
Evaluation Reference
```

# 18. AI Ranking

AI ranking may learn relationships between signals that are difficult to encode manually.

Conceptually:

```text
Eligible Candidates
        ↓
Feature Vector
        ↓
Ranking Model
        ↓
Candidate Scores
        ↓
Sorted Candidates
```

---

# 19. Scoring

A conceptual score may combine multiple signals:

```text
Candidate Score =
    f(
        ETA,
        Distance,
        Driver State,
        Ride Context,
        Demand,
        Supply,
        Historical Signals,
        Policy
    )
```

The exact scoring function must be defined by the selected implementation.

This document does not prescribe a specific ML algorithm.

---

# 20. Weighted Deterministic Scoring

An intermediate implementation may use a weighted score.

Conceptually:

```text
Score =
    w1 * ETA Score
  + w2 * Distance Score
  + w3 * Availability Score
  + w4 * Demand/Supply Signal
```

Weights must be measurable and configurable where appropriate.

This can provide a useful baseline before introducing a trained ranking model.

---

# 21. Predictive Ranking

A predictive ranking model may estimate:

```text
Expected Assignment Quality
```

or another explicitly defined objective.

The model should be trained against outcomes that represent actual dispatch quality.

---

# 22. Ranking Objective Must Be Explicit

Do not train a ranking model against an ambiguous target.

Define:

```text
What does "better driver" mean?
```

Possible objectives include:

```text
Lower Pickup ETA
Higher Acceptance Probability
Higher Completion Probability
Lower Cancellation Risk
Better Overall Dispatch Outcome
```

The objective should be chosen based on product and operational goals.

---

# 23. Multi-Objective Ranking

Future Smart Dispatch may optimize multiple objectives.

For example:

```text
Pickup ETA
+
Acceptance Probability
+
Cancellation Risk
+
Fleet Balance
```

A multi-objective score must have explicit policy and measurable trade-offs.

---

# 24. Avoiding Single-Metric Optimization

Optimizing only:

```text
Distance
```

may produce poor outcomes.

Optimizing only:

```text
ETA
```

may also produce undesirable fleet behaviour.

The ranking objective should therefore be evaluated against the complete operational outcome.

---

# 25. Driver Availability

Availability is a hard operational constraint.

A driver who is not available should not be ranked simply because a model predicts high assignment quality.

Preferred flow:

```text
Availability Check
        ↓
Eligible
        ↓
Ranking
```

# 28. Demand-Aware Dispatch

Demand prediction may provide context about future ride demand.

Conceptually:

```text
Current Ride
+
Future Demand
+
Current Supply
        ↓
Dispatch Ranking
```

This may help balance:

```text
Immediate Pickup Quality
+
Future Supply Availability
```

This is an advanced strategy and should be experimentally validated.

---

# 29. Supply-Aware Dispatch

Supply information may influence candidate ranking.

Example:

```text
Candidate A:
Fast pickup
but critical to a low-supply area

Candidate B:
Slightly slower
but supply is abundant in that area
```

A policy may choose B if the expected system-level outcome is better.

Such behaviour must be explicitly defined rather than hidden inside a model.

---

# 30. Dispatch Policy

The ranking model should operate within an explicit dispatch policy.

Policy may define:

```text
Hard Constraints
Soft Preferences
Optimization Objectives
Fallback

```

---

# 31. Hard Constraints

Hard constraints must never be violated.

Examples:

```text
Driver Must Be Eligible
Region Must Be Allowed
Driver Must Be Available
Ride Constraints Must Be Satisfied
Reservation Must Be Valid
```

AI cannot override these.

---

# 32. Soft Preferences

Soft preferences may include:

```text
Lower ETA
Lower Distance
Better Expected Outcome
Supply Balance
Driver Utilization
```

AI and ranking algorithms may optimize these.

---

# 33. Tie Breaking

When candidates have effectively equivalent scores, use deterministic tie-breaking.

Possible criteria:

```text
Lower ETA
Lower Distance
Stable Candidate ID Ordering
Queue Position
```

The exact rule should be defined by dispatch policy.

---

# 34. Score Stability

Small changes in input should not cause unreasonable ranking instability.

Monitor for:

```text
Frequent Rank Flapping
Large Score Changes
Unstable Candidate Ordering
```

when relevant.

---

# 35. Ranking Confidence

Where the model provides a meaningful quality/confidence signal, it may be used to determine whether AI ranking should be trusted.

Conceptually:

```text
High Confidence
→ AI Ranking

Low Confidence
→ Fallback / Conservative Ranking
```

Confidence must not be invented or interpreted without model-specific validation.

---

# 36. Model Output Validation

The system must validate:

```text
Candidate IDs
Scores
Score Range
Rank Ordering
Model Version
```

Invalid output must trigger the defined fallback.

---

# 37. Reservation Boundary

After ranking:

```text
Top Candidate
        ↓
Reservation Attempt
```

The reservation operation must be authoritative.

The candidate can become unavailable between ranking and reservation.

---

# 38. Reservation Race

Consider:

```text
Request A
    ↓
Ranks Driver X

Request B
    ↓
Ranks Driver X
```

Both may attempt reservation.

The core transactional reservation mechanism must resolve the race.

AI ranking cannot guarantee exclusive ownership.

---

# 39. Reservation Failure

If the top-ranked candidate cannot be reserved:

```text
Top Candidate
      ↓
Reservation Failure
      ↓
Next Candidate
```

subject to the active dispatch policy.

---

# 40. Retry Strategy

Retries should be bounded.

Avoid:

```text
Retry Forever
```

Use:

```text
Bounded Attempts
+
Candidate Progression
+
Final Fallback
```

---

# 41. Dispatch Timeout

Smart Dispatch must operate within the ride request latency budget.

The system should define:

```text
Candidate Discovery Timeout
Feature Resolution Timeout
AI Inference Timeout
Reservation Timeout
Overall Dispatch Timeout
```

---

# 42. AI Inference Timeout

AI inference must have a strict timeout.

If the timeout is exceeded:

```text
Inference Timeout
        ↓
Fallback
```

The ride request must not wait indefinitely for an AI prediction.

---

# 43. Smart Dispatch Fallback

Fallback must preserve the resolved dispatch strategy unless an explicit business/configuration rule defines a strategy change.

For Smart Dispatch:

```text
AI Ranking
    ↓ failure
Deterministic Smart Ranking
    ↓ failure
Configured Smart Dispatch Fallback
```

For Smart Stand Dispatch:

```text
AI Ranking / Advanced Ranking
    ↓ failure
Deterministic Stand-aware Prioritization
    ↓
Broader candidate discovery where configured
```

A broader candidate search does **not** mean:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch → Smart Dispatch
```

The exact fallback hierarchy is governed by the active dispatch policy and must be observable.


---

# 44. Fallback Must Be Tested

Fallback is not an emergency-only concept.

It must be tested for:

```text
Model Unavailable
Inference Timeout

Invalid Model Output
```

# 45. AI Service Failure

If the AI service becomes unavailable:

```text

    X
     ↓
Core Dispatch
     ↓
Fallback
     ↓
Ride Continues
```

---

# 46. Feature Failure

If a required feature cannot be resolved:

```text
Missing Feature
      ↓
Can Safe Default Be Used?
      ├── Yes → Continue
      └── No  → Fallback
```

Do not silently inject arbitrary values.

---

# 47. Model Version Failure

If the requested model version cannot be loaded:

```text
Model Version Failure
        ↓
Approved Alternative Model
        ↓
or
        ↓
Deterministic Fallback
```

Only approved model versions should be used.

# 57. Local vs Global Optimization

A local objective:

```text
Best Driver for This Ride
```

differs from:

```text
Best Assignment for Fleet-Level Outcome
```

---

# 58. Dispatch Strategy by Operating Context

The platform may support:

```text
Context A
→ Smart Stand Dispatch

Context B
→ Smart Dispatch

Context C
→ Smart Dispatch with Advanced / AI-assisted Ranking
```

Strategy selection is resolved through the hierarchical configuration model.

Possible configuration levels include:

```text
State
District
City / Town
Rural Area
Auto Stand
Specific Ride Level
Other configured intermediate levels
```

Not every level requires explicit configuration.

The rule is:

```text
Most Specific Applicable Level
        ↓
Explicit Strategy?
   ├── YES → Use it
   └── NO  → Move to Parent
        ↓
Continue upward
        ↓
System Default if none exists
```

A more specific explicit configuration overrides an inherited higher-level configuration.

Strategy selection should be explicit, deterministic, and observable.

---

# 59. Strategy Selection Safety

AI should not silently switch dispatch strategies.

The effective dispatch strategy must be resolved from the configured hierarchy before dispatch execution.

Any automatic strategy selection or policy-driven strategy override must have:

```text
Defined Policy
Approved Conditions
Observability
Fallback
Rollback
```

Candidate expansion also must not be interpreted as strategy switching.

For example:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
    ↓
Preferred stand has no suitable driver
    ↓
Expand to nearby/non-stand/cross-location candidates
```

still remains:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
```

unless an explicit configuration or business rule defines another strategy.

```text
+

+
Fallback-Safe
```

# 101. Canonical Dispatch Strategy Model

This section is authoritative for the AI-assisted dispatch behavior described by this document.

## 101.1 Strategy Resolution

The effective dispatch strategy must be resolved before AI-assisted ranking or strategy-specific dispatch execution.

```text
Ride Request
    ↓
Applicable Configuration Hierarchy
    ↓
Nearest Explicit Dispatch Configuration
    ↓
Effective Dispatch Strategy
```

The hierarchy may include:

```text
State
→ District
→ City / Town
→ Rural Area
→ Auto Stand
→ Specific Ride Level
→ Other configured intermediate levels
```

The hierarchy is not required to be fully configured.

The canonical rule is:

> **The most specific explicitly configured dispatch strategy takes precedence.**

If no explicit strategy exists at a level, resolution continues to the parent. If no explicit strategy exists anywhere in the applicable hierarchy, the system default is used.

AI must consume the resolved strategy; it must not independently reinterpret the hierarchy.

---

## 101.2 Smart Stand Dispatch

Smart Stand Dispatch is:

> **Stand-preferred, not stand-exclusive.**

When the rider is inside a configured auto-stand radius:

```text
Ride Pickup
    ↓
Preferred Auto Stand
    ↓
Eligible Stand Drivers
    ↓
Stand Queue / Ordering
```

The system first considers suitable eligible drivers from that stand.

If the stand cannot provide a suitable driver, the candidate flow may expand to:

```text
Drivers outside the stand
Nearby stands
Drivers from nearby locations
```

The broader candidate set remains subject to deterministic eligibility, geographic reachability, legal/regional rules, service constraints, and the active dispatch policy.

If the rider is outside all configured stand radii:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
    ↓
No Applicable Stand Preference
    ↓
Eligible Nearby Drivers
```

Stand membership must not become a hard candidate boundary.

---

## 101.3 Smart Dispatch

Smart Dispatch is stand-agnostic.

It considers eligible nearby drivers without using auto-stand membership as an inherent preference.

Candidates may include:

```text
Drivers at auto stands
Drivers outside auto stands
Drivers from nearby locations
```

provided they satisfy the applicable constraints.

---

## 101.4 Cross-Location Dispatch

A dispatch request must not stop merely because the origin location has no suitable driver.

Candidate discovery may expand to nearby locations.

Example:

```text
Location A
Smart Dispatch
    ↓
No suitable local driver
    ↓
Expand search
    ↓
Location B
Smart Stand Dispatch
    ↓
Evaluate eligible B candidates
```

The source location's strategy context must be preserved.

For example, if Location B uses Smart Stand Dispatch, its stand/queue context may affect how its candidates are prioritized.

However:

```text
Different Location
+
Different Dispatch Strategy
≠
Automatically Ineligible
```

The final candidate decision must still consider the same hard constraints and applicable dispatch factors.

---

## 101.5 Strategy Is Not Candidate Boundary

The following distinction is mandatory:

```text
Dispatch Strategy
        ≠
Candidate Discovery Boundary
```

Smart Stand Dispatch does not mean:

```text
Only stand drivers
```

Smart Dispatch does not mean:

```text
Only drivers from the origin location
```

Geographic expansion and candidate discovery determine which eligible candidates can enter the pipeline. The resolved dispatch strategy determines how those candidates are prioritized and processed.

---

## 101.6 AI-Assisted Dispatch Is an Optimization Layer

AI-assisted dispatch is not a third primary dispatch strategy.

The architecture is:

```text
Resolved Dispatch Strategy
        ↓
Strategy-specific Candidate Discovery
        ↓
Deterministic Eligibility
        ↓
Feature Resolution
        ↓
AI / Algorithmic Ranking
        ↓
Reservation
```

AI may assist with:

```text
ETA prediction
Acceptance prediction
Cancellation prediction
Candidate ranking
Demand/supply signals
Other validated optimization signals
```

AI must not override:

```text
Eligibility
Legal / Regional Rules
Safety Constraints
Service Constraints
Reservation State
Transactional Correctness
```

---

## 101.7 Strategy-Specific AI Ranking

AI ranking must respect the resolved strategy.

For Smart Dispatch:

```text
Eligible Candidates
    ↓
Normal Smart Ranking Signals
    ↓
AI-assisted Ranking
```

For Smart Stand Dispatch:

```text
Preferred Stand Candidates
    ↓
Stand Queue / Ordering Policy
    ↓
If insufficient suitable supply
    ↓
Broader Eligible Candidates
    ↓
AI-assisted Ranking where configured
```

The AI layer must not erase the business meaning of the stand queue.

If the business policy defines first-eligible-in-queue semantics, a generic AI score must not silently override that rule.

---

## 101.8 Candidate Context

The AI/ranking layer should receive enough context to distinguish candidates correctly.

Useful context may include:

```text
Effective Dispatch Strategy
Configuration Level
Origin Location
Candidate Location
Candidate Location Strategy
Stand Membership
Relevant Stand
Queue Position
Discovery Source
Expansion Level
Distance
ETA
Driver Availability
Eligibility Result
```

Not every field must be sent to every model. Only features required by the selected implementation should be used.

---

## 101.9 Fallback and Strategy Preservation

AI failure must degrade the ranking capability, not silently redefine the business strategy.

For example:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
    ↓
AI unavailable
    ↓
Deterministic stand-aware prioritization
```

and:

```text
Smart Dispatch
    ↓
AI unavailable
    ↓
Deterministic Smart Dispatch ranking
```

Candidate expansion may still occur according to the active strategy and discovery policy.

The following is prohibited unless explicitly configured:

```text
AI Failure
    ↓
Smart Stand Dispatch
    ↓
Silently become Smart Dispatch
```

---

## 101.10 AI-Agent Implementation Rules

An AI coding agent modifying Smart Dispatch AI must:

1. Resolve and preserve the effective dispatch strategy.
2. Treat Smart Stand Dispatch as stand-preferred, not stand-exclusive.
3. Never make stand membership an implicit hard eligibility rule.
4. Preserve cross-location candidates when they satisfy the applicable constraints.
5. Preserve source-location strategy context.
6. Keep deterministic eligibility before AI ranking.
7. Keep reservation authoritative and transactional.
8. Keep AI-assisted ranking separate from strategy resolution.
9. Avoid silently changing dispatch strategy during fallback.
10. Preserve stand queue semantics where required by business policy.
11. Use existing ETA, geo, candidate, and matching components where available.
12. Add deterministic fallback before introducing new ML dependencies.
13. Add model/output validation and bounded inference timeouts.
14. Add observability for strategy, candidate context, model version, ranking, and fallback.
15. Update the relevant ADR/business/architecture documents when the dispatch behavior itself changes.

---

## 101.11 Prohibited Implementations

Do not implement:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch = stand-only dispatch.

Pickup inside stand radius = reject every non-stand driver.

Nearby location = automatically ineligible.

Different location strategy = automatically ineligible.

Candidate expansion = automatic switch to Smart Dispatch.

AI ranking = authority over eligibility.

AI ranking = authority over reservation.

AI model = third dispatch strategy.

AI failure = silent strategy change.

Configuration hierarchy = hard-coded inside the AI service.

AI score = automatic override of stand queue policy.
```
