# RideForge — Ride Lifecycle

> **Document:** `docs/01-business/3.RIDE_LIFECYCLE.md`  
> **Status:** Active  
> **Scope:** Business lifecycle and state-transition rules for rides  
> **Related:** `1.DISPATCH_BUSINESS_RULES.md`, `2.DRIVER_LIFECYCLE.md`, `4.MATCHING_RULES.md`, `5.RETRY_POLICY.md`, `docs/00-project/5.TERMINOLOGY.md`

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the business lifecycle of a RideForge ride.

It establishes:

- the major ride states,
- valid state transitions,
- when a ride may enter dispatch,
- how matching interacts with the ride lifecycle,
- assignment and trip progression,
- cancellation and failure handling,
- lifecycle invariants.

This document defines business behaviour.

Database tables, Redis keys, Kafka topics, Go types, and other implementation mechanisms must implement these rules rather than redefine them.

---

# 2. Ride Lifecycle Overview

The high-level ride lifecycle is:

```text
REQUESTED
    ↓
MATCHING
    ↓
DRIVER_ASSIGNED
    ↓
DRIVER_ARRIVING
    ↓
DRIVER_ARRIVED
    ↓
TRIP_STARTED
    ↓
TRIP_COMPLETED
```

Cancellation or failure may terminate the ride from applicable intermediate states:

```text
REQUESTED ───────────────► CANCELLED
MATCHING ────────────────► CANCELLED
DRIVER_ASSIGNED ─────────► CANCELLED
DRIVER_ARRIVING ─────────► CANCELLED
DRIVER_ARRIVED ──────────► CANCELLED
```

The exact transition rules depend on the current ride state.

---

# 3. Core Ride States

The business lifecycle distinguishes these concepts:

```text
REQUESTED
MATCHING
DRIVER_ASSIGNED
DRIVER_ARRIVING
DRIVER_ARRIVED
TRIP_STARTED
TRIP_COMPLETED
CANCELLED
```

The implementation may use equivalent internal constants, but the business meaning must remain consistent.

---

# 4. REQUESTED

`REQUESTED` means the ride request has been created but has not yet been successfully assigned to a driver.

A requested ride may be waiting for:

```text
Dispatch
Validation
Matching
```

subject to the application flow.

---

# 5. Entering Matching

A ride enters `MATCHING` when the system begins an active attempt to find a suitable driver.

Conceptually:

```text
REQUESTED
    ↓
MATCHING
```

A ride must satisfy all applicable dispatch eligibility/business rules before normal matching begins.

---

# 6. Matching State

While a ride is in `MATCHING`, the dispatch system may:

```text
Discover candidates
Enrich candidates
Filter candidates
Rank candidates
Reserve drivers
Create offers
Wait for driver responses
Retry
```

The ride remains matchable until:

```text
Successful assignment
Cancellation
Terminal failure
```

according to business policy.

---

# 7. Candidate Discovery Does Not Assign the Ride

The existence of candidates does not change the ride to an assigned state.

The sequence remains:

```text
Candidates Found
      ↓
Ranked
      ↓
Reserved
      ↓
Offered
      ↓
Accepted
      ↓
Assigned
```

Only the appropriate successful assignment transition changes the ride's assignment state.

---

# 8. No Candidate

If no eligible candidate is found:

```text
MATCHING
    ↓
No Candidate
```

is a dispatch outcome, not automatically a ride failure.

The matching/retry policy determines whether another attempt should occur.

---

# 9. Reservation Failure

If candidates exist but reservation attempts fail:

```text
MATCHING
    ↓
Reservation Attempts
    ↓
No Successful Reservation
```

the ride remains subject to dispatch policy.

Reservation contention is not automatically a ride lifecycle failure.

---

# 10. Offer Rejection

If an offered driver rejects:

```text
Offer Rejected
    ↓
Release Reservation
    ↓
Continue Matching
```

when the ride is still matchable.

The ride should not be marked assigned.

---

# 11. Offer Timeout

If an offer expires without acceptance:

```text
Offer Timeout
    ↓
Release Reservation
    ↓
Continue Matching
```

when the ride remains eligible for dispatch.

Timeout is an unsuccessful offer outcome, not automatically a ride cancellation.

---

# 12. Successful Assignment

When a valid driver accepts and the application successfully establishes the assignment:

```text
MATCHING
    ↓
DRIVER_ASSIGNED
```

At this point the ride has an authoritative assigned driver.

---

# 13. Assignment Authority

The following are not sufficient to establish assignment:

```text
Candidate discovered
Candidate ranked
Driver reserved
Offer created
Offer delivered
Driver notification sent
```

Assignment requires the authoritative ride/driver state transition.

---

# 14. One Assigned Driver

A ride must not end up with multiple conflicting assigned drivers.

Concurrent dispatch operations must therefore be protected by appropriate application and persistence/concurrency controls.

---

# 15. Driver Arrival Lifecycle

After successful assignment, the driver progresses toward pickup:

```text
DRIVER_ASSIGNED
      ↓
DRIVER_ARRIVING
      ↓
DRIVER_ARRIVED
```

The exact operational triggers depend on the realtime/driver interaction layer.

---

# 16. DRIVER_ASSIGNED

`DRIVER_ASSIGNED` means a driver has been successfully assigned to the ride.

The ride should no longer be processed as an ordinary unassigned matching request.

The dispatch system may still perform assignment-related cleanup and event processing.

---

# 17. DRIVER_ARRIVING

`DRIVER_ARRIVING` means the assigned driver is actively travelling toward the pickup location.

The driver is no longer available for an independent normal dispatch assignment.

---

# 18. DRIVER_ARRIVED

`DRIVER_ARRIVED` means the assigned driver has reached the applicable pickup condition.

The ride remains active and awaits trip start.

---

# 19. TRIP_STARTED

`TRIP_STARTED` means the passenger trip has begun.

The ride transitions:

```text
DRIVER_ARRIVED
      ↓
TRIP_STARTED
```

The driver remains associated with this ride until completion or an applicable exceptional lifecycle.

---

# 20. TRIP_COMPLETED

`TRIP_COMPLETED` is the terminal successful ride state.

After completion:

```text
Ride
  → terminal

Driver
  → may return to AVAILABLE
```

provided the driver remains otherwise eligible and online.

---

# 21. CANCELLED

`CANCELLED` is a terminal unsuccessful ride state.

Once a ride is cancelled, normal matching must stop.

The system must clean up applicable:

```text
Reservations
Offers
Timeouts
Dispatch state
```

---

# 22. Cancellation Before Matching

A ride may be cancelled before dispatch starts:

```text
REQUESTED
    ↓
CANCELLED
```

No new matching operation should begin after the cancellation becomes authoritative.

---

# 23. Cancellation During Matching

A ride may be cancelled while matching is active:

```text
MATCHING
    ↓
CANCELLED
```

Any active reservation/offer associated with the ride must be cleaned up according to the applicable business and infrastructure rules.

---

# 24. Cancellation After Assignment

Cancellation after assignment is subject to the ride cancellation policy.

The system must not blindly apply the same cancellation behaviour used before assignment.

For example:

```text
Before assignment
    → stop matching

After assignment
    → handle assigned-driver cancellation rules
```

The detailed policy may depend on the current ride state.

---

# 25. Cancellation Is Terminal

Once the ride reaches:

```text
CANCELLED
```

it must not return to:

```text
MATCHING
DRIVER_ASSIGNED
TRIP_STARTED
```

through a normal lifecycle transition.

Any exceptional administrative recovery must be an explicit separate operation.

---

# 26. Completion Is Terminal

Once the ride reaches:

```text
TRIP_COMPLETED
```

normal dispatch must not restart.

A completed ride must not become:

```text
MATCHING
```

again.

---

# 27. Matching Eligibility

A ride may enter or remain in matching only while it is eligible for dispatch.

Conceptually:

```text
Ride is matchable
AND
Ride is not cancelled
AND
Ride is not already assigned
AND
Retry policy permits another attempt
```

---

# 28. Matching Attempt

A **Matching Attempt** represents one dispatch cycle for the ride.

An attempt may contain:

```text
Candidate Discovery
Candidate Pipeline
Ranking
Reservation
Offer
Response Waiting
```

A failed attempt does not necessarily terminate the ride.

---

# 29. Retry Between Attempts

When an attempt fails to produce assignment:

```text
Attempt N
   ↓
Retry Policy
   ↓
Attempt N+1
```

provided the ride remains matchable and the retry policy permits continuation.

---

# 30. Maximum Attempts

Matching must be bounded.

The ride must not remain in an infinite retry loop.

When the configured retry limit is reached:

```text
Stop normal matching
```

The resulting ride state must follow the applicable business policy.

---

# 31. Retry Is Not a New Ride

A retry represents another dispatch attempt for the same ride.

It must preserve:

```text
Ride Identity
Passenger
Pickup
Destination
Ride Lifecycle
```

while allowing dispatch parameters to change.

---

# 32. Dispatch Parameter Changes

Across retries, dispatch policy may change:

```text
Search Radius
Candidate Limit
Offer Batch Size
Offer Timeout
```

These are dispatch-policy changes, not ride-state changes.

---

# 33. Ride State and Dispatch State

Ride lifecycle state and dispatch attempt state must remain distinct.

For example:

```text
Ride:
MATCHING

Dispatch:
Attempt 3
Radius = X
Batch = Y
```

The dispatch attempt should not become the ride's business state.

---

# 34. Active Offers

While a ride is in matching, one or more active offers may exist according to dispatch policy.

The system must track active offers sufficiently to prevent:

```text
Duplicate Offers
Expired Offers Becoming Valid
Conflicting Assignment
```

---

# 35. Multiple Active Offers

If dispatch policy permits multiple offers:

```text
Ride
 ├── Driver A → Offer
 ├── Driver B → Offer
 └── Driver C → Offer
```

only one driver may ultimately become the successful assigned driver.

The assignment operation must be concurrency-safe.

---

# 36. First Valid Acceptance

When multiple valid offers exist, the system must prevent multiple successful assignments.

The first valid acceptance that successfully establishes the authoritative assignment wins according to the concurrency rules.

Other outstanding offers must then be invalidated or cleaned up.

---

# 37. Acceptance Race

If two drivers accept nearly simultaneously:

```text
Driver A → Accept
Driver B → Accept
```

the system must resolve the race through authoritative state/concurrency controls.

It must never produce:

```text
One Ride → Two Assigned Drivers
```

---

# 38. Stale Acceptance

A driver acceptance must be rejected if:

```text
Ride is already assigned
Offer is expired
Offer is cancelled
Ride is cancelled
Offer is otherwise invalid
```

A delayed network message must not override current authoritative state.

---

# 39. Ride Cancellation Race

Cancellation and driver acceptance may occur concurrently.

The system must resolve the race through authoritative state transitions.

The final state must not violate lifecycle invariants.

---

# 40. Driver Reservation Cleanup

When the ride leaves the matching lifecycle, any reservation that exists solely for its matching operation must be released when appropriate.

Examples:

```text
Successful Assignment
Ride Cancellation
Offer Timeout
Offer Rejection
Dispatch Failure
```

---

# 41. Timeout Cleanup

Timeout processing must be idempotent.

Repeated timeout processing must not:

```text
Assign the ride
Release unrelated reservations
Create duplicate retries
Create duplicate events
```

---

# 42. Event-Driven Ride Transitions

Important ride state changes may produce application events.

Examples:

```text
Ride Created
Ride Matching Started
Driver Offered
Driver Accepted
Driver Rejected
Offer Timed Out
Driver Assigned
Trip Started
Trip Completed
Ride Cancelled
```

Only events required by the architecture should be introduced.

---

# 43. Transactional State Changes

When a ride state transition must be accompanied by an event publication intent, the state change and outbox event should be committed consistently.

Conceptually:

```text
Database Transaction
 ├── Ride State Change
 └── Outbox Event
        ↓
      Commit
```

The event broker should not be treated as the transaction boundary.

---

# 44. Event Idempotency

Ride lifecycle event consumers must tolerate duplicate event delivery where applicable.

Repeated processing must not produce unintended duplicate state transitions.

---

# 45. Durable Ride State

The ride lifecycle is durable business state.

Realtime infrastructure may accelerate:

```text
Location
Offers
Timeouts
Dispatch
```

but the authoritative ride lifecycle must remain durable.

---

# 46. Realtime State Does Not Override Ride State

A websocket message, Redis value, or delayed event must not independently redefine the authoritative ride state.

For example:

```text
Driver sends "accept"
```

does not guarantee assignment until the application successfully applies the assignment transition.

---

# 47. Ride State Authority

The conceptual authority flow is:

```text
Ride Business State
       ↓
Dispatch State
       ↓
Realtime Delivery
```

Realtime delivery reflects application state; it does not automatically become the source of truth.

---

# 48. No-Candidate Outcome

When matching finds no eligible driver:

```text
MATCHING
   ↓
No Candidate
```

the ride remains subject to retry policy.

If retries are exhausted, the system follows the configured/business terminal outcome.

The absence of a candidate must remain distinguishable from infrastructure failure.

---

# 49. Infrastructure Failure

Infrastructure failure during matching is different from:

```text
No Candidate
```

Examples:

```text
Redis unavailable
Database failure
Event infrastructure failure
Routing provider failure
```

The application must preserve this distinction for retry and operational handling.

---

# 50. Candidate Pipeline Failure

If enrichment, filtering, or ranking fails due to an actual application/infrastructure error, the ride must not silently be treated as successfully matched or as a normal no-candidate result.

The failure should be observable and handled by application policy.

---

# 51. Partial Dispatch Failure

Dispatch can partially succeed.

Example:

```text
Driver A
  Reservation → Success
  Offer → Success

Driver B
  Reservation → Success
  Offer → Failure
```

The system must preserve successful state while cleaning up failed partial work.

---

# 52. Ride Assignment Cleanup

After successful assignment:

```text
Other active offers
      ↓
Invalidate / cleanup

Unused reservations
      ↓
Release

Pending matching work
      ↓
Stop
```

The ride must not continue ordinary candidate matching after assignment.

---

# 53. Driver Lifecycle Interaction

Ride and driver lifecycles are coupled but distinct.

For example:

```text
Ride:
MATCHING
   +
Driver:
AVAILABLE
   ↓
Reservation
   ↓
Driver:
RESERVED
   +
Ride:
MATCHING
   ↓
Acceptance
   ↓
Driver:
ASSIGNED / ON_TRIP
   +
Ride:
DRIVER_ASSIGNED / TRIP_STARTED
```

Neither lifecycle should be collapsed into the other.

---

# 54. Ride Completion and Driver Availability

When a ride completes:

```text
Ride → TRIP_COMPLETED
```

the driver may transition toward:

```text
AVAILABLE
```

only after applicable trip completion and driver-state processing.

---

# 55. Regional Rules

A ride's pickup and destination may fall into different operating regions.

The ride must pass the applicable regional/business eligibility rules before normal dispatch.

Geographic reachability alone does not determine permission.

---

# 56. Stand Dispatch

Under stand dispatch, the ride may be assigned according to stand/queue-specific business rules.

The ride lifecycle remains the same:

```text
Matching
 → Assigned
 → Trip
```

Only the selection mechanism differs.

---

# 57. Smart Dispatch

Under smart dispatch, candidate selection may use:

```text
H3
Availability
ETA
Quality
Fairness
Demand
```

The ride lifecycle remains independent of those ranking details.

---

# 58. Ride Lifecycle Invariants

The following invariants must always hold.

### Invariant 1

A cancelled ride must not receive a new normal assignment.

### Invariant 2

A completed ride must not re-enter normal matching.

### Invariant 3

A ride must not have two conflicting assigned drivers.

### Invariant 4

Candidate discovery does not constitute assignment.

### Invariant 5

Driver reservation does not constitute assignment.

### Invariant 6

Offer creation does not constitute assignment.

### Invariant 7

Only a valid active offer may produce an assignment.

### Invariant 8

Matching retries must remain bounded.

### Invariant 9

Late responses must not override authoritative current state.

### Invariant 10

Ride state changes requiring transactional event publication must preserve state/event consistency.

---

# 59. Invalid Transitions

Normal lifecycle processing must reject invalid transitions such as:

```text
CANCELLED → MATCHING
TRIP_COMPLETED → MATCHING
TRIP_COMPLETED → DRIVER_ASSIGNED
DRIVER_ASSIGNED → MATCHING
```

unless an explicit administrative/recovery operation defines such behaviour.

---

# 60. State Transition Authority

State transitions should be performed through the application service responsible for the ride lifecycle.

Handlers, realtime delivery, and event consumers must not bypass lifecycle rules.

---

# 61. State Transition Validation

Before applying a transition, the system should verify:

```text
Current State
+
Requested Transition
+
Required Preconditions
```

A transition that violates the lifecycle must be rejected rather than silently applied.

---

# 62. Ride Cancellation and Offers

When a ride becomes cancelled:

```text
Active Offers
      ↓
Invalidated / cleaned up

Reservations
      ↓
Released where applicable

Timeout Processing
      ↓
Must not assign the ride
```

Delayed driver responses must not revive the cancelled ride.

---

# 63. Ride Cancellation and Driver Assignment

If a ride is already assigned, cancellation follows the applicable post-assignment business rules.

The system must not reuse pre-assignment cancellation logic without checking the current ride state.

---

# 64. Ride Cancellation and Trip State

Cancellation after trip start is a different business event from cancellation before trip start.

If post-start cancellation is supported, it must have explicit lifecycle/business rules rather than being treated as an ordinary pre-dispatch cancellation.

---

# 65. Business Outcome Categories

Ride dispatch outcomes should remain distinguishable:

```text
Assigned
No Candidate
No Reservation
Offer Rejected
Offer Timed Out
Retry Exhausted
Cancelled
Infrastructure Failure
```

These outcomes support operations, metrics, and future dispatch optimization.

---

# 66. Ride Lifecycle Metrics

The lifecycle should support measurement of:

```text
Time to Matching
Time to Assignment
Matching Attempts
Candidates Discovered
Eligible Candidates
Reservations Attempted
Reservations Succeeded
Offers Created
Offers Accepted
Offers Rejected
Offers Timed Out
Cancellations
Trip Duration
```

Metrics should remain useful without introducing unnecessary high-cardinality labels.

---

# 67. Ride State and Observability

Important state transitions should be observable through:

```text
Metrics
Structured Logs
Traces
Events
```

Observability must describe the lifecycle rather than become a second lifecycle implementation.

---

# 68. AI Agent Implementation Rules

Before changing ride lifecycle behaviour, an AI coding agent must:

1. Read this document.
2. Read `1.DISPATCH_BUSINESS_RULES.md`.
3. Read `2.DRIVER_LIFECYCLE.md`.
4. Inspect the existing ride domain/state model.
5. Inspect ride application services.
6. Inspect matching orchestration.
7. Inspect event/outbox handling.
8. Identify existing state-transition guards.
9. Reuse existing lifecycle contracts.
10. Add or update tests for changed transitions and failure paths.

---

# 69. AI Agent Forbidden Patterns

An AI agent must not:

- introduce a second ride state machine without a clear responsibility boundary,
- change ride states merely to satisfy a roadmap phase,
- treat Redis/realtime state as authoritative ride lifecycle state,
- assign a ride merely because an offer was created,
- allow expired offers to assign a ride,
- allow cancelled rides to continue normal matching,
- allow completed rides to re-enter matching,
- bypass transactional state-transition logic,
- create duplicate event publication paths,
- turn infrastructure failures into ordinary no-candidate outcomes,
- remove concurrency protection around assignment.

---

# 70. Canonical Lifecycle

```text
REQUESTED
    ↓
MATCHING
    ↓
DRIVER_ASSIGNED
    ↓
DRIVER_ARRIVING
    ↓
DRIVER_ARRIVED
    ↓
TRIP_STARTED
    ↓
TRIP_COMPLETED
```

Cancellation may terminate applicable pre-terminal states:

```text
REQUESTED ───────────────► CANCELLED
MATCHING ────────────────► CANCELLED
DRIVER_ASSIGNED ─────────► CANCELLED*
DRIVER_ARRIVING ─────────► CANCELLED*
DRIVER_ARRIVED ──────────► CANCELLED*
```

`*` Post-assignment cancellation is governed by its own business rules.

The most important lifecycle distinctions are:

```text
Candidate ≠ Assignment
Reservation ≠ Assignment
Offer ≠ Assignment
Matching ≠ Assignment
Realtime Event ≠ Authoritative State
Cancellation ≠ Retry
Retry ≠ New Ride
```

These distinctions are mandatory business invariants.
