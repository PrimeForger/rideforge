# RideForge — Dispatch Business Rules

> **Document:** `docs/01-business/1.DISPATCH_BUSINESS_RULES.md`  
> **Status:** Active  
> **Scope:** Business rules governing ride dispatch and driver assignment  
> **Related:** `docs/00-project/1.PROJECT_VISION.md`, `2.SYSTEM_GOALS.md`, `3.ARCHITECTURE_PRINCIPLES.md`, `4.ROADMAP.md`, `5.TERMINOLOGY.md`

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the business rules governing how RideForge determines which drivers may be considered, selected, reserved, and offered a ride.

It describes **business behaviour**, not implementation details.

The implementation may use:

- H3,
- Redis,
- PostgreSQL,
- event-driven processing,
- ranking,
- routing,
- reservation locks,

but these technologies must implement the rules defined here rather than redefine them.

---

# 2. Dispatch Objective

The primary dispatch objective is:

> Select and successfully assign an eligible driver to a ride while balancing geographic suitability, travel time, driver quality, fairness, operational policy, and system reliability.

The system should optimize for successful ride assignment rather than simply selecting the geographically closest driver.

---

# 3. Dispatch Flow

The business-level dispatch flow is:

```text
Ride Becomes Matchable
        ↓
Validate Dispatch Eligibility
        ↓
Discover Candidate Drivers
        ↓
Enrich Candidate Information
        ↓
Filter Ineligible Candidates
        ↓
Rank Eligible Candidates
        ↓
Select Offer Candidates
        ↓
Reserve Driver
        ↓
Create Driver Offer
        ↓
Wait for Response
        ↓
Accept / Reject / Timeout
        ↓
Assign or Continue Dispatch
```

Each step has a distinct responsibility.

---

# 4. Ride Must Be Dispatchable

A ride must satisfy the applicable ride/business conditions before matching begins.

A ride that is:

```text
Cancelled
Completed
Already Assigned
Otherwise Not Matchable
```

must not enter normal driver matching.

The exact ride lifecycle states are defined in:

```text
docs/01-business/3.RIDE_LIFECYCLE.md
```

---

# 5. Driver Eligibility

A driver may be considered only when the driver satisfies the applicable eligibility rules.

At minimum, dispatch should distinguish:

```text
Driver exists
Driver is active
Driver is operationally available
Driver is not already reserved
Driver is not already offered for the same ride
Driver satisfies applicable business restrictions
```

Additional eligibility rules may be introduced when required by the operating model.

---

# 6. Availability Is Not Reservation

A driver appearing available does not guarantee that the driver can be assigned.

The business distinction is:

```text
Available
    ↓
Eligible Candidate
    ↓
Reservation
    ↓
Offer
```

Another ride may reserve the same driver after candidate discovery.

Therefore, successful reservation is required before the driver is treated as claimed for the dispatch operation.

---

# 7. Reservation Is the Assignment Gate

Reservation is the authoritative concurrency gate for dispatch.

The system must not assume that:

```text
Candidate discovered
```

means:

```text
Driver assigned
```

Only successful reservation permits the system to proceed with the corresponding offer.

---

# 8. Geographic Candidate Discovery

Candidate discovery should prioritize drivers geographically relevant to the pickup location.

The preferred business behaviour is:

```text
Pickup Location
      ↓
Nearby Driver Search
      ↓
Candidate Population
```

The system should not scan the entire driver population for ordinary dispatch.

---

# 9. Progressive Geographic Expansion

If sufficient eligible candidates are not available in the initial geographic area, dispatch may expand the search.

Conceptually:

```text
Local Area
   ↓
Enough candidates?
   ├── Yes → continue
   └── No
        ↓
Wider Area
        ↓
Enough?
        ├── Yes → continue
        └── No → continue / fail according to policy
```

The search must remain bounded by configured policy.

---

# 10. Driver Density

The geographic search may adapt to local driver density.

Conceptually:

```text
Dense Area
    → Prefer narrower search

Sparse Area
    → Expand search more aggressively
```

The purpose is to avoid wasting work in areas where sufficient drivers are already nearby while maintaining useful coverage in sparse areas.

---

# 11. Candidate Population

Candidate discovery produces a **candidate population**, not a final assignment.

The candidate population may contain drivers who later fail:

```text
Validation
Availability
Already-offered
Business eligibility
Ranking
Reservation
```

This is expected.

---

# 12. Candidate Deduplication

A driver must not appear multiple times as an independent candidate for the same matching operation.

If the same driver is discovered through multiple geographic cells or search paths, it should be represented once in the candidate set.

---

# 13. Candidate Enrichment

Candidates should receive the information required to make eligibility and ranking decisions.

Where multiple candidates require the same backend data source, the system should prefer batch retrieval.

The business requirement is:

```text
Obtain enough reliable driver information
to make a dispatch decision.
```

---

# 14. Candidate Filtering

Candidates must pass the applicable filters before being considered for dispatch.

The filtering concept is:

```text
Discovered
   ↓
Valid?
   ↓
Unique?
   ↓
Available?
   ↓
Not already offered?
   ↓
Eligible
```

Filters should eliminate candidates that cannot currently receive the ride.

---

# 15. Already-Offered Rule

A driver who already has an active offer for the same ride should not receive another independent offer for that same dispatch context.

This prevents duplicate offers and ambiguous driver state.

---

# 16. Driver Reservation Rule

When a driver is selected for an offer:

```text
Candidate
   ↓
Reserve
```

If reservation fails because another dispatch operation has already claimed the driver:

```text
Do not create the offer.
Continue with another candidate.
```

A normal reservation race should not be treated as a successful assignment.

---

# 17. Reservation Failure Classification

The system must distinguish between:

### Reservation rejected

The driver could not be claimed because the reservation condition was not satisfied.

This is an expected dispatch outcome.

### Reservation error

The reservation mechanism failed operationally.

This indicates an infrastructure/application failure and may require different handling.

---

# 18. Ranking Principle

Eligible drivers should be ranked using meaningful dispatch signals.

The preferred long-term ranking model is:

```text
ETA / Travel
+
Driver Quality
+
Fairness
+
Demand / Context
```

Distance alone should not be treated as the final dispatch decision.

---

# 19. ETA Preference

When reliable routing information is available, estimated arrival time should be preferred over straight-line geographic distance.

Example:

```text
Driver A
Distance = 2 km
ETA = 10 min

Driver B
Distance = 3 km
ETA = 6 min
```

Driver B may be preferable because the dispatch objective is timely pickup rather than minimum geometric distance.

---

# 20. Driver Quality

Driver quality may contribute to ranking.

Relevant signals may include:

```text
Acceptance Rate
Cancellation Rate
Timeout Rate
Rating
Completed Trips
```

Quality must not override fundamental eligibility requirements.

---

# 21. Fairness

Dispatch should avoid repeatedly selecting the same eligible drivers when meaningful alternatives exist.

Fairness may consider:

```text
Idle Time
Time Since Last Assignment
```

Fairness is a ranking consideration, not an eligibility override.

A driver must not be selected solely because they have waited longer if they are otherwise unsuitable.

---

# 22. Demand-Aware Dispatch

The dispatch system should be capable of considering local supply and demand.

Potential context:

```text
Driver Supply
Ride Demand
Geographic Cell
```

This is intended to improve overall dispatch efficiency and future demand-aware behaviour.

Demand-aware signals must not bypass driver eligibility or reservation rules.

---

# 23. Ranking Configuration

Ranking weights are policy.

They should be configurable rather than hidden inside ranking calculators.

Conceptually:

```text
Feature
   ↓
Normalize
   ↓
Configured Weight
   ↓
Final Score
```

The exact production weights belong to configuration and may evolve based on measured outcomes.

---

# 24. Offer Selection

After ranking, the dispatch policy determines how many candidates should receive offers.

The number may be adaptive.

Conceptually:

```text
Candidate Ranking
       ↓
Dispatch Policy
       ↓
Offer Batch
```

The matching engine should execute the policy rather than hard-code the business decision.

---

# 25. Parallel Offer Consideration

Multiple candidates may be selected for an offer batch when dispatch policy permits.

For each selected candidate:

```text
Reserve
  ↓
Persist Offer Attempt
  ↓
Publish Offer Event
```

Only successfully reserved drivers should receive offers.

---

# 26. Offer Creation

An offer should represent a real dispatch opportunity.

The offer should only be created after the required reservation and persistence conditions succeed.

The system should avoid creating an offer for a driver that was not successfully reserved.

---

# 27. Offer Timeout

Every offer must have a bounded validity period.

If the driver does not respond within the applicable timeout:

```text
Offer
   ↓
Timeout
   ↓
Release / Cleanup
   ↓
Continue Dispatch
```

The timeout should be controlled by dispatch policy.

---

# 28. Driver Acceptance

When a driver accepts a valid offer:

```text
Offer
   ↓
Accepted
   ↓
Assignment / Ride Progression
```

The system must ensure that only a valid active offer can produce a successful acceptance.

---

# 29. Driver Rejection

When a driver rejects an offer:

```text
Offer
   ↓
Rejected
   ↓
Release Reservation
   ↓
Continue Dispatch
```

The ride should remain matchable when the ride lifecycle permits another attempt.

---

# 30. Offer Timeout and Rejection

Both timeout and rejection represent unsuccessful offers.

However, they are different outcomes:

```text
Rejected
    = Driver explicitly declined

Timed Out
    = Driver did not respond within the allowed period
```

The distinction should be preserved for metrics, driver quality calculations, and future dispatch policy.

---

# 31. Successful Assignment

A dispatch operation is successful when a valid driver accepts and the system establishes the appropriate ride-driver assignment state.

The system should not report a successful match merely because:

```text
Candidate found
Driver ranked
Driver reserved
Offer created
```

Acceptance/assignment state determines the successful business outcome.

---

# 32. Multiple Offers

Multiple simultaneous offers may be used according to dispatch policy.

The system must prevent multiple drivers from ultimately becoming the confirmed driver for the same ride.

The assignment operation must therefore remain concurrency-safe.

---

# 33. Concurrent Matching

The dispatch system must assume that multiple operations can attempt to match drivers concurrently.

Examples:

```text
Two rides → same driver
Two matching workers → same ride
Retry → previous offer still active
```

Business correctness must be preserved under these conditions.

---

# 34. Same-Ride Concurrent Matching

A ride should not be allowed to create conflicting dispatch attempts that result in inconsistent assignment state.

Idempotency and ride-state checks must protect the ride lifecycle.

---

# 35. Cross-Ride Driver Competition

A driver may be discovered by multiple rides.

The system resolves the competition at reservation.

Conceptually:

```text
Ride A ──┐
         ├── Reserve Driver
Ride B ──┘
```

Only one operation should successfully obtain the reservation.

---

# 36. Matching Attempts

Matching attempts should remain bounded.

A ride must not retry indefinitely.

The applicable maximum is controlled by dispatch configuration/policy.

When the retry limit is reached:

```text
Stop normal matching
```

and transition according to ride/business policy.

---

# 37. Retry Expansion

Retries may change dispatch parameters such as:

```text
Search Radius
Candidate Limit
Offer Batch Size
Offer Timeout
```

The retry policy determines the values.

The matching engine should not embed retry formulas directly.

---

# 38. Retry Is Not Blind Repetition

A retry should not simply repeat the exact same operation without considering the current dispatch context.

The policy may adapt based on:

```text
Attempt Count
Nearby Driver Count
Eligible Driver Count
Previous Outcome
```

Future policy improvements may use acceptance probability and other historical signals.

---

# 39. No-Candidate Outcome

If no suitable candidate is found:

```text
No Candidate
```

is a normal dispatch outcome.

It is not automatically an infrastructure failure.

The system should follow retry/ride policy.

---

# 40. No-Reservation Outcome

If candidates exist but none can be reserved:

```text
Candidate Found
      ↓
Reservation Attempts
      ↓
None Successful
```

this is a dispatch outcome distinct from candidate discovery failure.

The system may retry according to policy.

---

# 41. Discovery Failure

If the candidate discovery infrastructure fails:

```text
Candidate Search Error
```

this is different from:

```text
No Drivers Found
```

The distinction is important for retry decisions, observability, and operational diagnosis.

---

# 42. Ranking Failure

If ranking cannot be completed because required data or computation fails, the system should not silently treat the result as a valid ranked candidate set.

The failure should remain observable and follow application error handling.

---

# 43. Partial Offer Failure

When multiple candidates are being offered, one candidate may succeed while another fails.

The system must preserve the state of successful operations and clean up failed partial operations where necessary.

Example:

```text
Driver A
Reserve → Success
Offer persistence → Success

Driver B
Reserve → Success
Offer persistence → Failure
        ↓
Release Driver B
```

---

# 44. Reservation Cleanup

If an offer cannot be created after a successful reservation, the reservation must be released when appropriate.

This prevents leaked driver reservations.

---

# 45. Offer Lifecycle Authority

Offer state must be determined by the application's durable offer/ride state and lifecycle rules.

Realtime delivery state must not silently redefine the durable business outcome.

---

# 46. Event Reliability

Important offer and dispatch state transitions that require asynchronous communication should use the transactional outbox mechanism.

Business state and the corresponding outbox event must remain transactionally consistent where required.

---

# 47. Duplicate Event Handling

Duplicate events must not create duplicate business effects.

Examples:

```text
Driver Accepted Event
Driver Rejected Event
Offer Timeout Event
```

must be processed idempotently.

---

# 48. Stale Offer Handling

An offer that is no longer valid must not be accepted as a current offer merely because a delayed response arrives.

The application must verify the offer/ride state before applying the response.

---

# 49. Driver State After Successful Assignment

Once a driver is successfully assigned to a ride, the driver must no longer be treated as generally available for another independent ride assignment.

The exact driver lifecycle is defined in:

```text
docs/01-business/2.DRIVER_LIFECYCLE.md
```

---

# 50. Geographic and Business Eligibility

Geographic proximity does not automatically mean that a ride is permitted.

The system must distinguish:

```text
Geographically Nearby
```

from:

```text
Business / Legally Eligible
```

Regional operating rules must be evaluated through the appropriate business/application rule.

---

# 51. Cross-Region Dispatch

Cross-region rides must follow the applicable regional/business restrictions.

The dispatch engine must not assume that all geographically reachable regions are operationally interchangeable.

---

# 52. Dispatch Strategy Model

RideForge supports two primary dispatch strategies:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
Smart Dispatch
```

These are distinct business strategies, but they share the common dispatch pipeline and hard eligibility/concurrency rules.

The selected strategy is determined by the applicable dispatch configuration before strategy-specific dispatch behaviour is executed.

The system must not treat the dispatch strategy itself as a hard geographic candidate boundary.

---

# 53. Smart Stand Dispatch

**Smart Stand Dispatch** is a stand-preferred dispatch strategy.

When a ride request is created, the system first determines whether the pickup location resolves to the Smart Stand Dispatch strategy.

If the rider is within the configured radius of a relevant auto stand:

```text
Ride Request
    ↓
Rider inside Auto Stand Radius
    ↓
Identify Relevant Stand
    ↓
Consider Eligible Drivers at the Stand
    ↓
Apply Stand Queue / Ordering Rules
```

The stand is the **preferred dispatch source**, not an exclusive candidate pool.

If suitable drivers cannot be obtained from the preferred stand, the system may expand the candidate search to:

```text
Drivers from Nearby Stands
Drivers Outside Any Stand
Drivers from Nearby Locations
```

subject to the normal eligibility, geographic, legal, service, and dispatch constraints.

---

# 54. Smart Stand Dispatch — Rider Inside Stand Radius

When the rider is inside a configured auto stand's radius, the relevant stand should receive dispatch preference.

The business flow is:

```text
Rider
  ↓
Inside Stand Radius
  ↓
Preferred Stand
  ↓
Check Stand Drivers
  ↓
Skip Drivers Who Are Not Eligible / Available
  ↓
Apply Stand Queue / Ordering
```

The first suitable driver according to the stand's queue rules should be preferred.

The queue is therefore a strategy-specific prioritization mechanism.

It must not be interpreted as:

```text
Only drivers at the stand may receive the ride.
```

---

# 55. Smart Stand Dispatch — Stand Fallback

If the rider is within a stand radius but the preferred stand cannot provide a suitable driver, dispatch must not stop solely because the stand is unavailable.

The system may expand the candidate search according to the configured search policy.

Conceptually:

```text
Preferred Stand
      ↓
Suitable Driver?
 ├── YES → Continue with preferred stand candidate
 └── NO
      ↓
Expand Candidate Search
      ↓
Nearby Stands
      ↓
Non-Stand Drivers
      ↓
Nearby Locations
      ↓
Evaluate Eligible Candidates
```

A driver outside the preferred stand may therefore receive the ride when the preferred stand cannot provide a suitable driver.

---

# 56. Smart Stand Dispatch — Rider Outside All Stand Radii

If the rider is outside the radius of all configured auto stands, Smart Stand Dispatch must not create a stand-only candidate restriction.

The system should consider eligible nearby drivers normally.

The candidate pool may include:

```text
Stand Drivers
Non-Stand Drivers
Drivers from Nearby Locations
```

subject to the same eligibility and geographic constraints applicable to dispatch.

In this case there is no relevant stand from which to apply stand preference.

---

# 57. Smart Dispatch

**Smart Dispatch** is the stand-agnostic dispatch strategy.

When Smart Dispatch is the effective strategy for the ride, candidate discovery and ranking do not use auto-stand membership as a dispatch preference.

The system considers eligible nearby drivers based on the existing dispatch factors, including:

```text
Driver Eligibility
Driver Availability
Distance
ETA
Matching / Ranking Factors
Operational Constraints
```

An eligible driver may be considered regardless of whether the driver is:

```text
At an Auto Stand
Outside an Auto Stand
Associated with Another Nearby Location
```

---

# 58. Dispatch Strategy Does Not Define Candidate Boundary

The following distinction is mandatory:

```text
Dispatch Strategy
    ≠
Candidate Discovery Boundary
```

The strategy determines how discovered candidates are prioritized and processed.

Candidate discovery determines where eligible candidates may be found.

Therefore:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
```

does not mean:

```text
Only Stand Drivers
```

and:

```text
Smart Dispatch
```

does not mean:

```text
Only Drivers Belonging to the Origin Location
```

Geographic expansion may introduce candidates from nearby locations when local supply is insufficient.

---

# 59. Hierarchical Dispatch Strategy Configuration

The effective dispatch strategy must be configurable through a hierarchy of configuration levels.

Possible configuration levels include:

```text
State
District
City / Town
Rural Area
Auto Stand
Specific Ride Level
Other Intermediate Configuration Levels
```

Not every level requires an explicit dispatch configuration.

The system must support inheritance from higher configuration levels.

---

# 60. Configuration Inheritance Rule

The canonical rule is:

> **The most specific explicitly configured dispatch strategy takes precedence over inherited configuration.**

When a ride is created, the system should:

```text
1. Identify the applicable configuration node.
2. Start at the most specific / lowest applicable level.
3. Check for an explicit dispatch strategy.
4. If found, use it.
5. Otherwise move to the parent configuration level.
6. Continue until an explicit strategy is found.
7. If no strategy exists in the hierarchy, use the system default.
```

Conceptually:

```text
Most Specific Level
        ↓
Explicit Strategy?
   ├── YES → Effective Strategy
   └── NO
        ↓
Parent Level
        ↓
Explicit Strategy?
   ├── YES → Effective Strategy
   └── NO
        ↓
Continue Upward
        ↓
System Default
```

---

# 61. Configuration Example

Suppose:

```text
District
    → Smart Dispatch

Town A
    → Smart Stand Dispatch

Town B
    → No Explicit Configuration
```

Then:

```text
Ride from Town A
    → Smart Stand Dispatch

Ride from Town B
    → Smart Dispatch
      (inherited from District)
```

The District configuration must not override Town A because Town A contains a more specific explicit configuration.

---

# 62. Configuration Hierarchy Must Be Extensible

The implementation must not assume a permanently fixed list such as:

```text
State → District → Town → Stand
```

The hierarchy should be represented as configurable parent-child relationships so additional levels can be introduced when required.

For example:

```text
State
  ↓
District
  ↓
Town
  ↓
Rural Area
  ↓
Auto Stand
  ↓
Ride-level Configuration
```

may be valid, while another deployment may use a different set of intermediate levels.

The business rule is the inheritance relationship, not a hard-coded geographic depth.

---

# 63. Configuration Is Separate from Driver Eligibility

Dispatch configuration determines:

```text
Which Dispatch Strategy Applies
```

It does not automatically determine:

```text
Which Drivers Are Eligible
```

For example:

```text
Town A
→ Smart Stand Dispatch
```

does not mean:

```text
Only Town A Stand Drivers
```

may serve the ride.

Candidate eligibility and geographic expansion remain separate dispatch concerns.

---

# 64. Cross-Location Dispatch

Cross-location dispatch occurs when the originating location cannot provide a suitable driver and the candidate search expands into nearby locations.

Example:

```text
Location A
→ Smart Dispatch

No suitable driver in A
        ↓
Expand search
        ↓
Location B
→ Smart Stand Dispatch
```

Drivers from Location B may be considered if they are otherwise eligible and able to serve the ride.

The dispatch strategy configured for Location B determines the strategy-specific prioritization/context applied when considering B's drivers.

The ride does not need to be reassigned to Location B merely because a B driver is considered.

---

# 65. Cross-Location Strategy Evaluation

When candidates are discovered from another location, the candidate's originating/configured location may have a different dispatch strategy.

For example:

```text
Location A
→ Smart Dispatch

Location B
→ Smart Stand Dispatch
```

If a ride originates in A and B is included in the expanded search:

```text
Location B
    ↓
Apply B's Smart Stand Dispatch context
    ↓
Evaluate eligible B drivers
    ↓
Return candidates to the ride's dispatch flow
```

The final candidate comparison remains for the original ride.

The system should ultimately select the best eligible driver according to the applicable dispatch rules.

---

# 66. Cross-Location Example

Suppose:

```text
Location A
→ Smart Dispatch

Location B
→ Smart Stand Dispatch
```

A ride originates in Location A.

There are no suitable eligible drivers in A.

The expanded search discovers:

```text
Location B — Stand Driver
Distance: 2 km
ETA: 5 min

Location B — Non-Stand Driver
Distance: 4 km
ETA: 9 min
```

Both drivers may be eligible.

The stand driver may be preferred because:

```text
B uses Smart Stand Dispatch
+
The driver is in the preferred stand context
+
The driver has better dispatch suitability
```

The fact that the ride originated in Location A does not by itself make the B driver ineligible.

---

# 67. Cross-Location Legal and Business Constraints

Cross-location discovery must never bypass legal or business eligibility.

Geographic proximity does not imply operational permission.

Before assignment, the system must still enforce:

```text
Regional / Legal Restrictions
Service Eligibility
Driver Eligibility
Vehicle Eligibility
Availability
Other Business Constraints
```

The applicable regional/legal rules remain authoritative.

---

# 68. Candidate Discovery and Strategy Execution

The dispatch architecture should maintain a conceptual separation between:

```text
Candidate Discovery
```

and:

```text
Strategy-Specific Prioritization
```

Candidate discovery may answer:

```text
Which drivers could potentially serve this ride?
```

Strategy execution may answer:

```text
Which eligible candidate should be preferred?
```

This separation allows:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
```

to prefer a stand candidate without excluding all non-stand candidates from later fallback.

---

# 69. Shared Rules Across Strategies

The following rules apply regardless of the selected dispatch strategy:

```text
Ride must be dispatchable
Driver must be eligible
Driver must be available
Candidate must satisfy business restrictions
Reservation must be successful before offer
Assignment must remain concurrency-safe
Offer lifecycle must remain valid
Retries must be bounded
Events must remain reliable
```

Only strategy-specific candidate prioritization and search behaviour may differ.

---

# 70. Ranking and Strategy Interaction

Ranking signals such as:

```text
ETA
Distance
Driver Quality
Fairness
Demand / Supply Context
```

remain applicable where the strategy requires ranking.

For Smart Stand Dispatch, stand preference and queue ordering are applied according to stand business rules before or alongside the applicable broader candidate evaluation.

The exact implementation of ranking is defined by the ranking architecture and must not weaken hard eligibility constraints.

---

# 71. AI-Assisted Dispatch

AI may assist either dispatch strategy where explicitly enabled.

AI may provide:

```text
ETA Prediction
Acceptance Prediction
Ranking Signals
Demand / Supply Prediction
Other Optimization Signals
```

AI does not independently select a third dispatch strategy.

The effective strategy remains:

```text
Smart Stand Dispatch
```

or:

```text
Smart Dispatch
```

AI is an optimization layer applied within that strategy.

---

# 72. AI Must Not Override Hard Constraints

AI-assisted ranking must not bypass:

```text
Legal Restrictions
Driver Eligibility
Ride Eligibility
Availability
Reservation Correctness
Safety Constraints
Authoritative Domain State
```

A model prediction is a ranking/optimization input, not a replacement for hard business rules.

---

# 73. No Candidate Outcome

If no suitable candidate is found after the applicable search and expansion policy:

```text
No Candidate
```

is a normal dispatch outcome.

The system should follow the configured retry and ride policy.

The system must not claim that a candidate was unavailable merely because the initial location had no local driver if the configured search policy permits further geographic expansion.

---

# 74. Candidate Discovery Failure

The system must distinguish:

```text
No Drivers Found
```

from:

```text
Candidate Discovery Infrastructure Failure
```

The former is a business dispatch outcome.

The latter is an operational/system failure.

This distinction remains important for retry, observability, and incident diagnosis.

---

# 75. Dispatch Strategy Resolution Failure

If dispatch configuration cannot be resolved because the configuration subsystem is unavailable or returns an invalid state, the system must not silently select an arbitrary strategy.

The system should follow the configured system-default/error policy.

If a valid system default is explicitly defined, it may be used according to that policy.

---

# 76. Strategy Configuration Changes

Changes to dispatch strategy configuration should affect newly resolved rides according to configuration version/effective-time rules.

An in-flight dispatch operation should not unexpectedly switch strategy merely because an administrator changed a configuration while the dispatch operation was already running, unless explicitly designed to do so.

---

# 77. Dispatch Configuration Consistency

A ride should have a deterministically resolved effective strategy for a dispatch attempt.

The system should be able to determine:

```text
Effective Strategy
Configuration Source
Configuration Level
```

for observability and debugging.

---

# 78. No Global Driver Broadcast

Normal dispatch should not broadcast a ride request to the entire driver population.

Candidate discovery must narrow the driver population using:

```text
Geographic Relevance
Business Eligibility
Availability
Configured Search Policy
```

The strategy may then apply its specific prioritization.

---

# 79. Candidate Limit

Candidate discovery should operate under a configured candidate limit.

The limit exists to:

- bound work,
- control memory,
- control ranking cost,
- reduce infrastructure load,
- maintain predictable latency.

The limit must not prevent required fallback/expansion behaviour when the configured policy permits additional search.

---

# 80. Search Budget

Geographic search should operate within a bounded search budget.

The system must not expand indefinitely when supply is unavailable.

The search budget should account for:

```text
Maximum Search Radius
Maximum Location Expansion
Maximum Candidate Count
Maximum Search Time
```

where applicable.

---

# 81. Offer Batch Limit

The number of simultaneous offers should be bounded by dispatch policy.

This prevents excessive:

```text
Driver notifications
Reservation contention
Offer state
Response traffic
```

---

# 82. Timeout Must Be Bounded

Driver offers must not remain active indefinitely.

The applicable timeout is determined by dispatch policy.

Future policy may adapt timeout based on:

```text
ETA
Traffic
Driver response history
```

---

# 83. Quality and Fairness Must Not Override Eligibility

Ranking signals are secondary to hard eligibility.

The order is:

```text
Eligibility
    ↓
Strategy-Specific Prioritization / Ranking
    ↓
Reservation
```

A high-quality or highly idle driver who is unavailable must not be selected.

---

# 84. Reservation Must Not Be Used as Filtering

Reservation is not a replacement for normal candidate filtering.

The system should filter obvious ineligible candidates before attempting reservation.

Reservation exists to establish authoritative ownership under concurrency.

---

# 85. Dispatch Should Prefer Successful Outcomes

When evaluating dispatch behaviour, the most important outcome is:

```text
Successful Driver Assignment
```

rather than:

```text
Maximum Number of Offers
```

The system should avoid unnecessary offers that do not improve assignment probability.

---

# 86. Operational Cost Rule

Dispatch decisions should avoid unnecessary expensive operations.

Preferred:

```text
Cheap geographic narrowing
        ↓
Candidate filtering
        ↓
Expensive travel / ETA
        ↓
Strategy-specific prioritization
        ↓
Ranking
```

rather than applying expensive calculations to every possible driver.

# 89. Rule Priority

When rules conflict, the following priority should generally apply:

```text
1. Legal / Business Eligibility
2. Ride Lifecycle Correctness
3. Driver Lifecycle Correctness
4. Reservation / Concurrency Correctness
5. Dispatch Strategy and Policy
6. Strategy-Specific Prioritization / Ranking Preference
7. Performance Optimization
```

A lower-priority optimization must not override a higher-priority correctness rule.

# 90. Business Rule vs Implementation

The following are business rules:

```text
Driver must be eligible.
Ride must be matchable.
The effective dispatch strategy must be resolved from applicable configuration.
Specific configuration overrides inherited configuration.
Smart Stand Dispatch is stand-preferred, not stand-exclusive.
Smart Dispatch is stand-agnostic.
Cross-location candidates may be considered when local supply is insufficient.
Reservation is required before offer.
Offer must expire.
Retry must be bounded.
Cross-region restrictions apply.
```

The following are implementation mechanisms:

```text
Redis
H3
PostgreSQL
Kafka
Lua
Heap
Go interfaces
```

Implementation mechanisms may change while the business rules remain stable.

# 91. Rule Change Principle

When a business rule changes:

```text
Update the business rule
        ↓
Identify affected policy/component
        ↓
Update implementation
        ↓
Update tests
        ↓
Update architecture documentation
        ↓
Update ADR if architecturally significant
```

Do not silently encode business-rule changes inside infrastructure.

---

# 92. AI Agent Requirements

Before changing dispatch behaviour, an AI coding agent must:

1. Read this document.
2. Read the relevant lifecycle documents.
3. Read the dispatch architecture document.
4. Read the applicable dispatch strategy and configuration ADRs.
5. Inspect the existing matching and configuration implementation.
6. Determine the effective dispatch strategy and configuration source relevant to the requested change.
7. Determine whether the requested behaviour already exists.
8. Reuse the existing policy/interface where appropriate.
9. Preserve reservation and transaction guarantees.
10. Add or update tests for changed business behaviour.
11. Update documentation when the business rule changes.

---

# 93. AI Agent Forbidden Patterns

An AI agent must not:

- treat Smart Stand Dispatch as a stand-only candidate pool,
- treat Smart Dispatch as a stand-based strategy,
- hard-code the configuration hierarchy into a fixed list of geographic levels,
- ignore configuration inheritance,
- allow a parent configuration to override a more specific explicit configuration,
- use dispatch strategy as a hard candidate boundary,
- prevent cross-location candidates solely because their location uses a different strategy,
- bypass the reservation abstraction,
- create offers for unreserved drivers,
- treat availability as ownership,
- add duplicate candidate/ranking systems,
- introduce a new dispatch abstraction when the existing one is sufficient,
- silently change regional eligibility rules,
- turn infrastructure failures into normal no-candidate results,
- remove idempotency protections,
- introduce roadmap-driven complexity without a requirement.

# 94. Business Rule Summary

The core RideForge dispatch flow is:

```text
Ride becomes matchable
        ↓
Resolve effective dispatch strategy
        ↓
Determine applicable candidate discovery policy
        ↓
Discover geographically relevant drivers
        ↓
Enrich candidates
        ↓
Filter ineligible candidates
        ↓
Apply strategy-specific prioritization
        ↓
Rank / compare candidates where applicable
        ↓
Select according to dispatch policy
        ↓
Reserve driver
        ↓
Create offer
        ↓
Wait for valid response
        ↓
Accept → assign
Reject / Timeout → release and continue
```

For Smart Stand Dispatch:

```text
Rider inside stand radius
        ↓
Prefer relevant stand queue
        ↓
If no suitable stand driver
        ↓
Expand to broader eligible candidates
```

For Smart Dispatch:

```text
Find eligible nearby drivers
        ↓
Rank using applicable dispatch factors
        ↓
Best eligible driver
```

For cross-location dispatch:

```text
No suitable local driver
        ↓
Expand to nearby locations
        ↓
Apply each candidate location's applicable strategy context
        ↓
Evaluate all eligible candidates
        ↓
Select the best eligible driver
```

The system must preserve:

```text
Eligibility
Configuration Inheritance
Strategy Correctness
Concurrency Safety
Bounded Search
Bounded Retry
Reliable Events
Correct Lifecycle State
Legal / Regional Compliance
```

while continuously improving:

```text
ETA Quality
Driver Quality
Fairness
Demand Awareness
Dispatch Success Rate
Latency
```
